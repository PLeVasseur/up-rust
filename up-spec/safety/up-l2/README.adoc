= Safety at the communication layer (uP-L2)
:toc: preamble

The key words "*MUST*", "*MUST NOT*", "*REQUIRED*", "*SHALL*", "*SHALL NOT*", "*SHOULD*", "*SHOULD NOT*", "*RECOMMENDED*", "*MAY*", and "*OPTIONAL*" in this document are to be interpreted as described in https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/bcp14[IETF BCP14 (RFC2119 & RFC8174)]

Conventions, layout, tags and status words: xref:../README.adoc[the safety area's README].

This part is the safety counterpart of xref:../../up-l2/README.adoc[the Communication Layer (uP-L2)] and of xref:../../up-l2/dispatchers/README.adoc[its dispatchers].
It holds two things:

* interaction: what the element guarantees for an RPC call and for the identity of a message;
* forwarding: what the Streamer guarantees when it carries messages between transports, and what a deployment provides to operate it.

Every item is still Draft.

== Whom this part binds

Where an item is filed says which layer it concerns.
It does not say that every SDK must meet it (xref:../README.adoc#filing[filing an item does not widen it]).

* *Written for one element.*
The items here were written for the up-rust SEooC (xref:../seooc-up-rust/README.adoc[its README]), whose parts include the Streamer's data plane.
They are not protocol requirements of uP-L2, and they bind no SDK or dispatcher of another language.
* *Bound by tag.*
Each requirement binds the part its tag names (xref:../README.adoc#tags[the tags]): the element (`SafetyUpRustCore`) or the Streamer (`SafetyStreamer`).
* *Assumptions of use.*
The two assumptions of use filed here are that element's, about operating its Streamer.
They bind an integrator that claims its safety-assurance scope (xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#who[who the assumptions bind]).
* *The protocol stays in force.*
Nothing here replaces or weakens a requirement of uP-L2 or of its dispatchers.

== What stays with the element

Some rules reach this layer and are still filed with the element, because they are rules of its own modes, memory, build and reports.
They are in xref:../seooc-up-rust/README.adoc[the element's part]:

* QoS, TTL and deadlines of a route (xref:../seooc-up-rust/qos.adoc[`qos.adoc`]);
* refusals, errors and route reports (xref:../seooc-up-rust/errors.adoc[`errors.adoc`]);
* the check of a role's resource ID against the protocol's ranges, made with the validation of deployment data (`+req~ssr-config-resource-ranges~1+`);
* the asynchronous interface's contract (`+req~ssr-async-seam~3+`);
* the Streamer's crate and its build (`+req~ssr-crate-streamer-isolated~3+`, `+req~ssr-build-panic-abort~3+`, `+req~ssr-build-specified-functions-only~1+`);
* the deferred end-to-end protection wire, with the hops a protected frame survives (`+req~ssr-e2e-survives-hops~1+` in xref:../seooc-up-rust/e2e-wire.adoc[`e2e-wire.adoc`]).

A carrier route that crosses integrity levels upward is a transport-layer rule: `+req~ssr-integrity-carrier-crossing~2+` in xref:../up-l1/integrity.adoc[integrity levels].
