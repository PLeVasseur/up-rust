= uP-L1 safety: What a deployment provides for its transports
:toc: preamble

The key words "*MUST*", "*MUST NOT*", "*REQUIRED*", "*SHALL*", "*SHALL NOT*", "*SHOULD*", "*SHOULD NOT*", "*RECOMMENDED*", "*MAY*", and "*OPTIONAL*" in this document are to be interpreted as described in https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/bcp14[IETF BCP14 (RFC2119 & RFC8174)]

Conventions and tags: xref:../README.adoc[the safety area's README].
This layer, and whom its items bind: xref:README.adoc[safety at uP-L1].
Scope and element boundary: xref:../seooc-up-rust/README.adoc[the up-rust SEooC's README].

NOTE: These are the three assumptions of use of the up-rust SEooC whose duty is about every transport: who can reach a transport's resources, which integrity level each transport is given, and which bindings and wires a deployment uses.

* Whom they bind: xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-assumptions-bind[An assumption of use binds the integrator who claims the scope, and replaces no requirement].

[[aou-seooc-peer-access]]
== Only authorized processes reach the transport

[.specitem,oft-sid="aou~seooc-peer-access~7",oft-title="Only authorized processes reach the transport",oft-needs="uman",oft-tags="SafetyManual,SafetyGateUpRust"]
--
The integrator *SHALL* restrict access to every transport's resources (shared-memory segments, sockets, service names) to the processes deployed to use them, with OS permissions or equivalent.

* *The fault model.* What a faulty peer within that restriction may do, and the boundary between safety and security, are stated in xref:../README.adoc#req-ssr-peer-fault-model[The requirements hold against a faulty peer].
* *Two-way shared state.* On a binding whose ports write shared state both ways, the integrator *SHALL* run every process on one service at one integrity level, and *SHALL* give a consumer at a lower level a copy route in place of a port on that service.
The refusal of any other port at setup, by name, and what a copy route protects are stated in xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader] and xref:../seooc-up-rust/runtime-modes.adoc#req-ssr-mode-init-failure[Initialization failure].
What one such binding adds is stated with that binding.
* *Zero-copy routes.* On a zero-copy route, the integrator *SHALL* ensure that the writer is developed to at least the reader's level, and that it never writes the storage of a frame that it has sent.
The integrator *SHALL* ensure the same on a serialized route on which the element decodes a frame in shared memory (xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader], "Decoding from shared memory").
* *Message IDs.* Message IDs are unique only to a stated residual (xref:../up-l2/interaction.adoc#req-ssr-ids-unique[Message IDs are unique]), and they are not secret, so the integrator *SHALL NOT* rely on them for authentication, in its applications or in its deployment.
* *Strict mode.* What strict mode's report lists of each route's declared peers and of the shared-memory permissions that its binding requests, and its warning of an ASIL receive route that admits undeclared peers, are stated in xref:../seooc-up-rust/build.adoc#req-ssr-strict-report[Strict mode reports on every run].
--

*Violation:*

* A malformed frame is refused, and its refusal returned or *counted*; on a route that declares its peers, a frame from an undeclared peer is refused and *counted* (xref:../seooc-up-rust/errors.adoc#req-ssr-refuse-no-panic[No panic on runtime paths]; xref:peers.adoc#req-ssr-peer-admission[Undeclared peers cannot lock out declared ones]).
* A racing write on a zero-copy route, or into a frame in shared memory while the element decodes it, is undefined behaviour and *undetectable* by the element.
* A peer at another level that the deployment lets onto a two-way service can crash or corrupt its peers' memory: *undetectable* by the element.
* A well-formed frame from a process that should have had no access, and a deliberate exploit, are *undetectable* by the element.

[horizontal]
ID:: `+aou~seooc-peer-access~7+`
ASIL:: B
ISO 26262:: 6-Annex D.2.4 (part-clause numbers only)

*Rationale.*
A publisher below the reader's level, or of unknown level, may write a sample while it is read, so the reader takes the copy route.
A zero-copy route relies on the writer instead, and a publisher that declares a level it was not built to is not caught at setup.

A shared reference cannot remain valid against an uncontrolled concurrent writer (source: the Rust Reference, Rust 1.98.1, chapter https://doc.rust-lang.org/1.98.1/reference/behavior-considered-undefined.html[Behavior considered undefined], rules undefined.race and undefined.alias), and bidirectional shared state also exposes a publisher to a faulty consumer.

[[aou-seooc-integrity-levels]]
== Each transport's integrity level is assigned correctly

[.specitem,oft-sid="aou~seooc-integrity-levels~8",oft-title="Each transport's integrity level is assigned correctly",oft-needs="uman",oft-tags="SafetyManual,SafetyGateUpRust"]
--
The integrator *SHALL* give each transport one integrity level in the deployment data: QM, A, B, C or D, ordered as xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-declared[Each transport declares one integrity level] states.
What the element does with that level, on every route of the transport and at setup, is stated in that requirement.
The integrator *SHALL* have each process declare the level that it was built to, and *SHALL* deploy at a level only a process that was developed to at least that level.

* *One binding's restrictions.* What one binding adds to the assignment is stated with that binding.
* *Above ASIL B.* The duties for data above ASIL B, and for the element inside a process or partition that holds software at ASIL C or D, are stated in xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-capability-ceiling[Data above ASIL B relies on measures outside the element].
* *Declared levels.* A declared level is a label: it neither raises the element's capability nor qualifies the process that declares it.
--

*Violation:*

* A route on which a reader would see shared memory written below the reader's level, or at an unknown level, is *refused at setup* by name, unless it takes the copy route (xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-declared[Each transport declares one integrity level]; xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader]).
* On a lending binding, a publisher whose level is below the level that its service records is *refused at setup*, at the publisher's own setup (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-integrity-level[A lending binding records and reports the writer's level]).
* On a binding whose ports write shared state both ways, a port whose level differs from the service's recorded level is *refused at setup* (xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader]).
* On a handoff binding the check does not apply, because the reader gets a private copy.
* A process that declares a level it was not built to is *undetectable* by the element.

[horizontal]
ID:: `+aou~seooc-integrity-levels~8+`
ASIL:: B
ISO 26262:: 6-7.4.8, 9-6 (part-clause numbers only)

*Rationale.*
A declared integrity level supports setup admission but cannot prove how a process was developed.

[[aou-seooc-qualified-bindings-and-wires]]
== Bindings and wires pass their contracts

[.specitem,oft-sid="aou~seooc-qualified-bindings-and-wires~9",oft-title="Bindings and wires pass their contracts",oft-needs="uman",oft-tags="SafetyManual,SafetyGateUpRust"]
--
The integrator *SHALL* use on the ASIL path only bindings and wires that pass the contract suites (the binding contract suite, the QoS contract suite and the wire contract suite) on the integrator's own target, at the exact versions and configuration whose evidence its supported configuration records.
The integrator *SHALL NOT* take evidence of one state for another, such as compiled, linked, exercised, source-reviewed, composition-verified or qualified, each as the qualification record of the binding or wire names its states (xref:../seooc-up-rust/README.adoc#req-ssr-verification-methods[The verification methods, and what a Verification line asks for]), and *SHALL NOT* take the evidence of one binding or wire for another.
For each combination of a wire and a binding that it uses on the ASIL path, the integrator *SHALL* use evidence of that combination, at its exact target, versions and configuration, and *SHALL NOT* take a wire's results on one binding for its results on another.

* *Pull routes with a deadline.* For a pull route that declares a deadline, the integrator *SHALL* use only a binding whose deadline checks of the QoS contract suite pass for each use that the deployment makes of the route: on a subscriber, and on a carrier ingress.
What such a binding reports and declares is stated in xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-arrival-reports[A binding reports each arrival within the latency that it declares, and each event as what it is], and what the element records, monitors and counts in xref:../seooc-up-rust/qos.adoc#req-ssr-qos-deadline[Deadlines are monitored] and xref:../seooc-up-rust/errors.adoc#req-ssr-error-no-silent-loss[No silent loss, and each loss counted once].
* *Response routes.* What a binding delivers on a response route that it serves by push is stated in xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-response-delivery[A binding delivers one response route from one thread at a time], and the element's checks of an arriving response, on a push route and on a pull route, in xref:../up-l2/interaction.adoc#req-ssr-rpc-check-at-arrival[A response completes its call only once its wire accepts it].
* *Cores without coherent atomics.* What the integrator uses for a route between cores whose atomics are not shown to be coherent with each other is stated in xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-target-environment[The target environment is verified] ("Several cores").

*Limits, deviations and known failures.*
The integrator *SHALL* accept for its deployment the limits that the qualification record of each binding that it uses states, and the deviations that it states, each within its scope and bounds and until it lapses (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-readme[A binding's qualification record states its limits and its failures]), and *SHALL NOT* take a known failure for either.
Where an assumption of one binding lists them, it lists that binding's known failures apart.

*`+unsafe+` code outside the element.*
A binding with `+unsafe+` code of its own, and a wire with hand-written `+unsafe+` code of its own, such as a wire over a C codec, are outside the element's `+unsafe+` inventory (xref:../seooc-up-rust/memory-safety.adoc#req-ssr-unsafe-inventory[Every `+unsafe+` is inventoried and justified]).
The integrator *SHALL* use one on the ASIL path only where that code is qualified: by the inventory and the evidence that its own qualification record gives (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-unsafe-inventory[A binding inventories its own `+unsafe+`]), or by a qualification that the integrator makes itself.

*The qualified list.*
The qualified list is the integrator's list of the bindings and wires that it has qualified as the first paragraph of this assumption asks, each at the exact version and configuration whose evidence its supported configuration records.
In the Report, Enforce and Locked states, the integrator *SHALL* have every binding and wire that it uses on its qualified list, and *SHALL* give strict mode that list and its acknowledgement of each deviation that a route of its deployment reaches, at the deviation's current revision.
What strict mode reports in its Report state, and refuses in its Enforce state, the Locked state included, for a binding or wire off the list, for a deviation whose acknowledgement is missing or stale and for a known failure, is stated in xref:../seooc-up-rust/build.adoc#req-ssr-strict-rules[The rules that strict mode checks], xref:../seooc-up-rust/build.adoc#req-ssr-strict-ack-revision[An acknowledgement names a deviation by ID and revision] and xref:../seooc-up-rust/build.adoc#req-ssr-strict-report[Strict mode reports on every run].
--

*Violation:*

* A wire that declares heap use on a native route, or a binding that declares middleware heap use without a waiver, is *refused at setup*.
* That a binding or wire keeps the contract that it declares, for example that its frame destructor frees nothing on a native route, is a limit of the contract: no requirement has the element check it at runtime, apart from the checks that a requirement names, such as that of a wire's writer (xref:../seooc-up-rust/memory-safety.adoc#req-ssr-unsafe-wire-untrusted[No wire can break the element's memory safety]).
* A deviation accepted here is not a waiver.
* In strict mode's Enforce state, the Locked state included, a binding or wire off the qualified list, each route within the scope of a deviation whose acknowledgement is missing or stale, and each route that a known failure affects, whatever is acknowledged, are *refused at setup*; in the Report state they are reported, and nothing is refused for them (xref:../seooc-up-rust/build.adoc#req-ssr-strict-rules[The rules that strict mode checks]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/build.adoc#req-ssr-strict-ack-revision[An acknowledgement names a deviation by ID and revision]).
* That a deviation accepted here has not lapsed is a limit of the contract: no requirement has a part check it, and strict mode judges whether an acknowledgement holds by the identifier and the revision of the deviation that it names (xref:../seooc-up-rust/build.adoc#req-ssr-strict-ack-revision[An acknowledgement names a deviation by ID and revision]).

[horizontal]
ID:: `+aou~seooc-qualified-bindings-and-wires~9+`
ASIL:: B

*Rationale.*
A declaration of capability needs target-specific contract evidence.
A deviation can be accepted within its exact scope and bounds, and a known failure cannot, however exactly it is bounded (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-readme[A binding's qualification record states its limits and its failures]).

Evidence attaches to the exact pins and configuration it was produced on.
A newer middleware release or a private variant does not inherit it, and neither does another binding.
Evidence that code compiles does not show that it runs, and a source review does not show its timing.
