= uP-L1 safety: What a deployment provides for its transports
:toc: preamble

The key words "*MUST*", "*MUST NOT*", "*REQUIRED*", "*SHALL*", "*SHALL NOT*", "*SHOULD*", "*SHOULD NOT*", "*RECOMMENDED*", "*MAY*", and "*OPTIONAL*" in this document are to be interpreted as described in https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/bcp14[IETF BCP14 (RFC2119 & RFC8174)]

Conventions, tags and status words: xref:../README.adoc[the safety area's README].
This layer, and whom it binds: xref:README.adoc[safety at uP-L1].
Scope and element boundary: xref:../seooc-up-rust/README.adoc[the up-rust SEooC's README].

These are the three assumptions of use of the up-rust SEooC whose duty is about every transport: who can reach a transport's resources, which integrity level each transport is given, and which bindings and wires a deployment uses.

* They are that element's assumptions, as `seooc` in their IDs says.
They bind an integrator that claims its safety-assurance scope, and no deployment of another SDK or transport (xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#who[who the assumptions bind]).
* They are deployment duties.
No software requirement of this layer is met by them, and they replace none.
* Each "Violation" text uses the three classes of xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#violation-classes[how a violation shows up].
* The safety manual of the SDK states them, and they carry `SafetyManual` and the gate tag `SafetyGateUpRust` (xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#coverage[tags and coverage]).
* What only one transport adds is stated with that transport: for iceoryx2, the two assumptions in xref:iceoryx2.adoc[the iceoryx2 page].
* The index of all the element's assumptions is in xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc[its assumptions of use].

The clauses in this table name a rule or a definition that another part states.
Each stays in its item because it qualifies that item's own duty, and the item governs.

== Only authorized processes reach the transport, and peers are faulty, never malicious

[.specitem,oft-sid="aou~seooc-peer-access~5",oft-title="Only authorized processes reach the transport, and peers are faulty, never malicious",oft-needs="uman",oft-tags="SafetyManual,SafetyGateUpRust"]
--
The integrator *SHALL* restrict access to every transport's resources (shared-memory segments, sockets, service names) to the processes deployed to use them, with OS permissions or equivalent.

* *The fault model.* Within that, this SEooC treats every peer as faulty, never malicious. What a faulty peer may do is defined once, for every part, as the faulty-peer model of the safety area's shared concepts. Only deliberate exploitation, such as engineering a peer-ID hash collision, is out of scope, as security.
* *Two-way shared state.* On a binding whose ports write shared state both ways, every process on one service runs at one integrity level; a lower-integrity consumer takes a copy route, and any other is refused at setup, by name (`+req~ssr-integrity-zero-copy~4+`). What one such binding adds is stated with that binding (for iceoryx2, `+aou~seooc-iceoryx2-level-separation~1+`).
* *Zero-copy routes.* On a zero-copy route, the element relies on the writer, developed to at least the reader's level, never writing a sample it has sent.
* *Message IDs.* Message IDs are unique only to a stated residual (`+req~ssr-ids-unique~4+`), and they are not secret, so nothing may rely on them for authentication.
* *Strict mode.* In strict mode, the report lists each route's declared peers and the shared-memory permissions its binding requests, and warns of an ASIL receive route that admits undeclared peers.
--

*Violation:*

* Malformed or undeclared frames are refused and *counted* (`+req~ssr-refuse-no-panic~1+`, `+req~ssr-peer-admission~2+`).
* A racing write on a zero-copy route is undefined behaviour and *undetectable*.
* A peer at another level that the deployment lets onto a two-way service can crash or corrupt its peers' memory: *undetectable* by the element.
* A well-formed frame from a process that should have had no access, and a deliberate exploit, are *undetectable*.

[horizontal]
ASIL:: B
Status:: Draft
ISO 26262:: 6-Annex D.2.4 (part-clause numbers only)

*Rationale.*
A faulty publisher writing to a sample while it is read is the copy route's case, and a lying publisher is not caught at setup.

A shared reference cannot remain valid against an uncontrolled concurrent writer, and bidirectional shared state also exposes a publisher to a faulty consumer.

_Why revision 5._
Revision 4 held three things besides the integrator's duties: the definition of a faulty peer, on which requirements of several parts rest; a restriction that only iceoryx2 has; and a refusal that it gave to the binding, while the requirement that states the refusal is allocated to the element.

This revision keeps the integrator's duties and their wording.
The definition is now stated once, as xref:../README.adoc#faulty-peer[the faulty-peer model], with the same content.
The iceoryx2 restriction is `+aou~seooc-iceoryx2-level-separation~1+`.
The refusal is cited to its requirement, which says who refuses.
That it is refused by name follows from `+req~ssr-mode-init-failure~4+`, under which every refusal at setup names its cause, the route where there is one, and the setter to change.
No duty is added, dropped or given to another party.

== Each transport's integrity level is assigned correctly

[.specitem,oft-sid="aou~seooc-integrity-levels~6",oft-title="Each transport's integrity level is assigned correctly",oft-needs="uman",oft-tags="SafetyManual,SafetyGateUpRust"]
--
The integrator *SHALL* give each transport one integrity level in the deployment data: QM, A, B, C or D, in that order. Every route on that transport carries the level (`+req~ssr-integrity-declared~3+`). Each process declares the level it was built to, and is developed to at least that level.

* *One transport's restrictions.* What one binding adds to the assignment is stated with that binding (for iceoryx2, `+aou~seooc-iceoryx2-level-separation~1+`).
* *Above ASIL B.* The duties for a level or for data above ASIL B are `+aou~seooc-capability-ceiling~1+`.
* *A level is a label.* A declared level is a label checked at setup: it neither raises the element's capability nor qualifies the process that declares it.
--

*Violation:*

* On a lending binding that records the writer's level in the shared definition, a writer below a zero-copy reader's level is *refused at setup*, naming the route.
* On a binding whose ports write shared state both ways, such as iceoryx2, a port whose level differs from the service's recorded level is *refused at setup* (`+req~ssr-integrity-zero-copy~4+`).
* On a handoff binding the check does not apply, because the reader gets a private copy.
* A lending binding that cannot record levels reports them as unknown, and an ASIL reader then gets the copy route.
* A process that declares a level it was not built to is *undetectable*.

[horizontal]
ASIL:: B
Status:: Draft
ISO 26262:: 6-7.4.8, 9-6 (part-clause numbers only)

*Rationale.*
A declared integrity level supports setup admission but cannot prove how a process was developed.

_Why revision 6._
Revision 5 held the assignment of a level, a sentence that only iceoryx2 needs, and the element's capability ceiling with the duties that follow from it.
They bind different deployments: the first every transport, and the second a deployment on iceoryx2.
The third binds only where data above ASIL B crosses the element, or where the element runs inside an ASIL C or D process or partition, whatever the level of its own data.

This revision keeps the assignment, with its wording, and is filed with the transports it concerns.
The iceoryx2 sentence is `+aou~seooc-iceoryx2-level-separation~1+`, and the ceiling and its duties are `+aou~seooc-capability-ceiling~1+`, with their wording.
No duty is added, dropped or given to another party.

== Bindings and wires pass their contracts

[.specitem,oft-sid="aou~seooc-qualified-bindings-and-wires~7",oft-title="Bindings and wires pass their contracts",oft-needs="uman",oft-tags="SafetyManual,SafetyGateUpRust"]
--
The integrator *SHALL* use on the ASIL path only bindings and wires that pass the contract suites (the binding contract suite, the QoS contract suite and the wire contract suite) on the integrator's own target, at the exact versions and configuration whose evidence its supported configuration records, and *SHALL NOT* take evidence of one state, compiled, linked, exercised, source-reviewed, composition-verified or qualified, for another.

* *32-bit targets.* On a 32-bit target, those are only the bindings and wires whose contracts pass on that target.
* *Pull bindings and deadlines.* A pull binding used for a route with a deadline reports an arrival through the runtime port only with evidence that a frame entered the route's queue, or that the queue's overflow policy discarded one, and within the arrival-report latency it declares in its QoS support. It never reports one on a wake hint alone. It reports each failure to read its own queue through the runtime port's receive-failure report. Its deadline checks of the QoS contract suite pass, on a subscriber and on a carrier ingress.
* *Response routes.* A push binding delivers one response route from one thread at a time, which the contract checks. A pull binding needs nothing: the element serializes the pulls of a response route itself.
* *Cores without coherent atomics.* A route between cores whose atomics are not coherent with each other (`+aou~seooc-target-environment~4+`) uses a transport whose qualification covers that.

The integrator accepts the limits and known anomalies that each binding's README states (`+req~ssr-binding-readme~1+`). For a deployment that uses iceoryx2, `+aou~seooc-iceoryx2-limits~1+` lists them, and lists apart that binding's known failures, which are not accepted limits.

A wire or binding from elsewhere with `+unsafe+` code of its own, such as a wire over a C codec, is a separate element, and the integrator qualifies its `+unsafe+` (`+req~ssr-unsafe-inventory~3+`). The bindings this project ships keep their own inventories under the same gate (`+req~ssr-binding-unsafe-inventory~1+`).

In strict mode, the Enforce state refuses a binding or wire that is not on the integrator's qualified list, an ASIL process that reaches a deviation not acknowledged at its current revision, and any known failure, whatever is acknowledged. The report lists each binding's README limits, deviations and known failures.
--

*Violation:*

* A wire that declares heap use on a native route, or a binding that declares middleware heap use without a waiver, is *refused at setup*.
* A binding or wire that breaks its contract while declaring that it keeps it, for example with a frame destructor that frees on a native route, is *undetectable* at runtime: the contract test is the only check.
* A known anomaly accepted here is not a waiver.
* In strict mode, a binding or wire off the qualified list, an unacknowledged deviation, and any known failure are *refused at setup* in the Enforce state.

[horizontal]
ASIL:: B
Status:: Draft

*Rationale.*
A declaration of capability needs target-specific contract evidence.
An accepted anomaly has a bounded justification, whereas an open known failure remains a failure.

Evidence attaches to the exact pins and configuration it was produced on.
A newer middleware release or a private variant does not inherit it, compiling is not running, and a source review is not a timing analysis.

_Why revision 6._
The element admits ordinary owning wires and gives a stronger resource guarantee only where a route requires it (`+req~ssr-heap-guarantees~1+`).
An item that relies on such a guarantee therefore has to ask for it, and that revision added that duty.
It also named the contract suites and the runtime port's reports by what they are, with no change to what a binding or wire must pass.

_Why revision 7._
Revision 6 held two things besides the duty to use only bindings and wires that pass their contracts: the duty to require a resource guarantee by name, which binds only an item that relies on one, and the limits, anomalies and known failures of one binding, iceoryx2.

This revision keeps the qualification duty, with its wording.
The duty to require a guarantee is `+aou~seooc-resource-guarantees~1+`, and the iceoryx2 lists are `+aou~seooc-iceoryx2-limits~1+`, each with its wording.
No duty is added, dropped or given to another party.
