= Safety at the transport layer (uP-L1)
:toc: preamble

The key words "*MUST*", "*MUST NOT*", "*REQUIRED*", "*SHALL*", "*SHALL NOT*", "*SHOULD*", "*SHOULD NOT*", "*RECOMMENDED*", "*MAY*", and "*OPTIONAL*" in this document are to be interpreted as described in https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/bcp14[IETF BCP14 (RFC2119 & RFC8174)]

Conventions, layout, tags and status words: xref:../README.adoc[the safety area's README].

This part is the safety counterpart of xref:../../up-l1/README.adoc[the Transport & Session Layer (uP-L1)].
It holds three things:

* what a transport and a wire guarantee;
* how shared memory is lent or copied between integrity levels;
* what a deployment provides for its transports.

Every item is still Draft.

== Whom this part binds

Where an item is filed says which layer it concerns.
It does not say that every transport must meet it (xref:../README.adoc#filing[filing an item does not widen it]).

* *Written for one element.*
The items here were written for the up-rust SEooC (xref:../seooc-up-rust/README.adoc[its README]) and for the transports and wires that work with it.
They are not protocol requirements of uP-L1, and they bind no transport or SDK of another language.
* *Bound by tag.*
Each requirement binds the part its tag names (xref:../README.adoc#tags[the tags]): every binding written against the element's contract (`SafetyBinding`, `SafetyBindingPull`), every wire written against it (`SafetyWire`), every serialized wire (`SafetyWireSerialized`), one binding alone, where it has a tag of its own (<<one-binding>>), or the element itself (`SafetyUpRustCore`).
* *Assumptions of use.*
The five assumptions of use filed here are that element's: three about every transport, and two about one transport, iceoryx2.
They bind an integrator that claims its safety-assurance scope (xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#who[who the assumptions bind]).
* *The protocol chapter stays in force for whoever follows it.*
No item here lifts a requirement of uP-L1, its security requirements included, from an implementation that follows it.
Where an item here differs from that chapter, or stands beside it with no stated relation, xref:../../contract-generations.adoc#transition[the transition table] says so: for an empty payload, for a native representation, for the iceoryx2 version, for a transport on LoLa and for the interface between an SDK and a transport.

== Words used in this part

These words are used as the items use them.
The items give them their meaning, and this list only points to it.

* A *binding* is a uP-L1 transport implementation, written against the element's lending or handoff contract.
It adapts the element to an underlying transport such as iceoryx2.
* A *wire* encodes and decodes a payload.
It is filed with this layer, beside the transports that carry what it encodes.
* A *lending* binding is one on which a reader can be given shared memory that a writer wrote (xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-declared[Each transport declares one integrity level]).
A *handoff* binding hands the element a private copy (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-integrity-level[A lending binding records and reports the writer's level]).
* A *zero-copy* route gives a reader a view into shared memory.
The *copy route* gives it a validated copy in storage the reader owns (xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader]).
* A binding with *two-way shared state* is one whose ports write shared state both ways (xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader]; xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-shared-state[A lending binding says which way its ports write shared state]).
* A *native* route carries a value as it lies in memory, and a *serialized* route carries an encoding of it in one stated format (xref:payload-paths.adoc#paths[the two kinds of route, side by side]).

== What is specific to one binding

The duties of every binding name no middleware (xref:bindings.adoc[allocated to each binding]), and neither do the assumptions about every transport (xref:deployment.adoc[what a deployment provides for its transports]).
What only one binding must meet, and what only a deployment on it provides, is stated with that binding, in items that are allocated to it alone.

[cols="2,3,5",options="header"]
|===
| Binding | Items of its own | What that means

| iceoryx2 | Three requirements (tag `SafetyIceoryx2`) and two assumptions of use, in xref:iceoryx2.adoc[the iceoryx2 page] | Its allowed panic paths, its static data segments and its service depth are stated there, with the limits, known anomalies and known failures that a deployment on it accepts or sees listed, and its separation of integrity levels
| LoLa | Six requirements (tag `SafetyLola`) and no assumption of use, in xref:lola.adoc[the LoLa page] | Both kinds of route, its counts and its storage, the boundary to LoLa's C++ code, the life of a subscription and of what LoLa is given, and LoLa's own assumptions of use are stated there, with what the general rules ask on LoLa. The LoLa binding is bound by the duties of every binding as well, and a deployment on it by the assumptions about every transport. No item of this area names an allowed panic path, a permit or an accepted limit for it. So it has no allowed panic path (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-no-panic[No panic on any peer input]), and its permits, limits and known anomalies are those that its README states (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-readme[The README says what the middleware cannot guarantee])
|===

* *No item of its own is not an exemption.*
A binding without a page is bound by every duty of the binding contract in full.
* *Evidence stays with its binding.*
A test that runs on one real transport is evidence on that transport, and none for another binding.
* *Another middleware.*
A binding on another middleware is bound the same way once it is written against the element's contract.
No item of this area names one.

== What stays with the element

Some rules reach a transport and are still filed with the element, because they are rules of the element's own modes, memory and reports.
A binding or wire author reads them in xref:../seooc-up-rust/README.adoc[the element's part]:

* the heap class that every wire, binding and route declares, and the stronger resource guarantees a route may require (xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-declared[The heap class is declared, with no default]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-guarantees[A stronger resource guarantee is required by name]);
* waivers for middleware allocation (xref:../seooc-up-rust/waivers.adoc#req-ssr-waiver-required[Middleware heap needs a named waiver]);
* QoS, TTL and deadlines of a route, which bind an L2 role's route and a carrier route alike (xref:../seooc-up-rust/qos.adoc#req-ssr-qos-no-downgrade[QoS is honored or refused, never weakened]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/qos.adoc#req-ssr-qos-deadline[Deadlines are monitored]).
They rest on a binding's declared capability and arrival latency, and on its middleware's own event deadlines being off on an ASIL deployment;
* release, the release service and pending cleanup (xref:../seooc-up-rust/release.adoc#req-ssr-release-lease-drop[A lease gives back its slot and nothing else]);
* memory safety against any wire and any binding written in safe code (xref:../seooc-up-rust/memory-safety.adoc#req-ssr-unsafe-wire-untrusted[No wire can break the element's memory safety]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/memory-safety.adoc#req-ssr-unsafe-binding-untrusted[No binding written in safe code can break the element's memory safety]), and the inventory of `+unsafe+` (xref:../seooc-up-rust/memory-safety.adoc#req-ssr-unsafe-inventory[Every `+unsafe+` is inventoried and justified]);
* the deferred end-to-end protection wire.
It is a wire, but it is the element's own safety mechanism and reaches RPC calls and Streamer hops too, so its items stay together in xref:../seooc-up-rust/e2e-wire.adoc[`e2e-wire.adoc`].
