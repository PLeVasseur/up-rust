= Safety at the transport layer (uP-L1)
:toc: preamble

The key words "*MUST*", "*MUST NOT*", "*REQUIRED*", "*SHALL*", "*SHALL NOT*", "*SHOULD*", "*SHOULD NOT*", "*RECOMMENDED*", "*MAY*", and "*OPTIONAL*" in this document are to be interpreted as described in https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/bcp14[IETF BCP14 (RFC2119 & RFC8174)]

Conventions, layout and tags: xref:../README.adoc[the safety area's README].

This part is the safety counterpart of xref:../../up-l1/README.adoc[the Transport & Session Layer (uP-L1)].
It holds three things:

* what a transport and a wire guarantee;
* how shared memory is lent or copied between integrity levels;
* what a deployment provides for its transports.

== Whom the items bind

NOTE: Where an item is filed says which layer it concerns (xref:../README.adoc#filing[filing and scope]).

* *One element.*
The items here were written for the up-rust SEooC (xref:../seooc-up-rust/README.adoc[its README]) and for the bindings and wires that work with it.
They are not protocol requirements of uP-L1, and none of them names a transport implementation of another SDK, or an SDK of another language.
* *Requirements.*
Each requirement's statement says whom it binds (xref:../README.adoc#req-ssr-requirements-bind[A requirement binds the actors to which it gives a duty]): such as every binding or those among them that it names, every wire, every serialized wire apart from the end-to-end protection wire, each wire of the XCDR2 little-endian appendable profile, one named binding (<<one-binding>>), or the element itself.
+
NOTE: Its tags say which repositories cover it (xref:../README.adoc#tags[the tags]).

* *Assumptions of use.*
The assumptions of use filed here are that element's: those about every transport (xref:deployment.adoc[what a deployment provides for its transports]), and those about one binding and its middleware (<<one-binding>>).
Whom they bind: xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-assumptions-bind[An assumption of use binds the integrator who claims the scope, and replaces no requirement].
* *The protocol chapter.*
The protocol chapter, xref:../../up-l1/README.adoc[the Transport & Session Layer (uP-L1)] with its transport pages, stays in force for what it covers until an adopted replacement takes effect for it (xref:../README.adoc#req-ssr-contract-transition[What a part declares and claims, and when a replacement takes effect]).

== Words used in this part

These words have the meaning that the items give them.

* A *binding*, the *binding contract*, a binding's *middleware* and *binding code*: xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-scope[What a binding is, and the binding contract].
* A *wire*, the *wire contract*, the *native wire* and a *serialized wire*: xref:wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-scope[What a wire is, and the wire contract].
Wires are filed with this layer, beside the bindings that carry what they write.
* A *lending* or a *handoff* binding: xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-scope[What a binding is, and the binding contract].
* A route that a binding serves by *push* or by *pull*, and a *pulled*, a *retained* and a *borrowed* frame: xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-scope[What a binding is, and the binding contract].
* A frame's *envelope*, and the envelope of a route or of a leg: xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-scope[What a binding is, and the binding contract].
* An *exact receiver count*: xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-receiver-count[A binding that declares an exact receiver count reports it for each request].
* A *zero-copy* route and the *copy route*: xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader].
* A binding with *two-way shared state*: xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-shared-state[A lending binding says which way its ports write shared state].
* A binding's *limits*, its *deviations* (known anomalies) and its *known failures*: xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-readme[A binding's qualification record states its limits and its failures].
* A *native* or a *serialized* route: xref:wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-scope[What a wire is, and the wire contract], and xref:payload-paths.adoc#paths[the two kinds of route, side by side].

== What is specific to one binding

The duties of every binding (xref:bindings.adoc[allocated to each binding]) and the assumptions about every transport (xref:deployment.adoc[what a deployment provides for its transports]) name no middleware.
What only one binding must meet, and what only a deployment on it provides, is stated with that binding, in items that are specific to it: they name no other binding, and one of them can give the element a duty of its own.

== Items filed with the element

NOTE: Some items that concern a transport or a wire are filed with the element, because they are rules of the element's own modes, memory and reports.
In their statements, some of these name a binding or a wire beside the element (xref:../README.adoc#req-ssr-requirements-bind[A requirement binds the actors to which it gives a duty]).

A binding or wire author reads them in xref:../seooc-up-rust/README.adoc[the element's part]:

* the heap class and the stronger resource guarantees (xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-declared[The heap class is declared, with no default]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-guarantees[A stronger resource guarantee is required by name, and admitted or refused]);
* waivers for middleware allocation (xref:../seooc-up-rust/waivers.adoc#req-ssr-waiver-required[Middleware heap needs a named waiver]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-waiver-approval[Waivers are approved with an interference argument]);
* QoS, TTL and deadlines of a route (xref:../seooc-up-rust/qos.adoc#req-ssr-qos-no-downgrade[QoS is honored or refused, never weakened]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/qos.adoc#req-ssr-qos-deadline[Deadlines are monitored]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/qos.adoc#req-ssr-qos-ttl[Expired messages are not processed]);
* losses and their counts (xref:../seooc-up-rust/errors.adoc#req-ssr-error-no-silent-loss[No silent loss, and each loss counted once]);
* the modes, startup and restart (xref:../seooc-up-rust/runtime-modes.adoc#req-ssr-mode-one-way[There is no way back]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/runtime-modes.adoc#req-ssr-mode-bounded-startup[Setup never waits without a bound]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/runtime-modes.adoc#req-ssr-mode-restart[A restart is an initialization]);
* release, the release service and pending cleanup (xref:../seooc-up-rust/release.adoc#req-ssr-release-none[Nothing is released in runtime mode]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/release.adoc#req-ssr-release-lease-drop[A lease gives back its slot and nothing else]);
* memory safety against any wire and any binding written in safe code (xref:../seooc-up-rust/memory-safety.adoc#req-ssr-unsafe-wire-untrusted[No wire can break the element's memory safety]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/memory-safety.adoc#req-ssr-unsafe-binding-untrusted[No binding written in safe code can break the element's memory safety]), and the inventory of `+unsafe+` (xref:../seooc-up-rust/memory-safety.adoc#req-ssr-unsafe-inventory[Every `+unsafe+` is inventoried and justified]);
* the end-to-end protection wire.
It is a wire, but it is the element's own safety mechanism and reaches RPC calls and Streamer hops too, so its items stay together in xref:../seooc-up-rust/e2e-wire.adoc[`e2e-wire.adoc`].

On the other pages of this layer, these items give a binding a duty as well: xref:payload-paths.adoc#req-ssr-payload-route-form[A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name], xref:payload-paths.adoc#req-ssr-payload-written-once[A payload is written once, into the storage that carries it] and xref:peers.adoc#req-ssr-native-abi-agreed[A native representation never crosses an ABI].
