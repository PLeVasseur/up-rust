= uP-L1 safety: Payload paths, end to end
:toc: preamble

The key words "*MUST*", "*MUST NOT*", "*REQUIRED*", "*SHALL*", "*SHALL NOT*", "*SHOULD*", "*SHOULD NOT*", "*RECOMMENDED*", "*MAY*", and "*OPTIONAL*" in this document are to be interpreted as described in https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/bcp14[IETF BCP14 (RFC2119 & RFC8174)]

Conventions, tags and status words: xref:../README.adoc[the safety area's README].
This layer, and whom it binds: xref:README.adoc[safety at uP-L1].
The two kinds of wire: xref:native-wire.adoc[the native wire] and xref:serialized-wires.adoc[serialized wires, and the values they own].
The duties of every binding: xref:bindings.adoc[allocated to each binding].

A payload reaches its reader on one of two kinds of route, native or serialized, over one of two kinds of binding, lending or handoff.
The choice of a wire and the choice of a transport are separate, so all four combinations exist.
A component that only carries a payload is a third case, and it reads nothing.

This page holds:

* the three requirements that hold on every path (tag `SafetyUpRustCore`): a payload is written once; a route is admitted, or refused for a named reason, and its form is fixed at setup; and a wire is given only a payload of its own encoding;
* the combinations of wire and transport (<<independent>>);
* who admits a route and who refuses one, with examples (<<admission>>);
* the two kinds of route side by side (<<paths>>);
* who owns what, and until when (<<lifetime>>);
* who is responsible for what (<<responsibility>>);
* carrying a payload, as distinct from reading it (<<carriage>>).

== Whom this page binds

* The three requirements bind the element.
* Each table names the rule that governs a cell, and that rule binds whoever its own item binds.
The tables add no duty.
* Nothing here binds a transport or a wire that is not written against the element's contract.

[[req-ssr-payload-written-once]]
== A payload is written once, into the storage that carries it

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-payload-written-once~1",oft-title="A payload is written once, into the storage that carries it",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore"]
--
*Sending.*
The element *MUST* have a payload written exactly once, directly into the storage that carries its frame:

* on a lending binding, the loan (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-lent-storage[Lent storage is exclusive, disjoint and stable for as long as it is held]);
* on a handoff binding, storage that the route reserved at setup.

That binds a native payload, which its sender initializes there (xref:native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-in-place[A native payload is written and read where it is carried, or its route is refused]), and a serialized payload, which its wire encodes there (xref:serialized-wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-encode-decode[A serialized wire encodes once into the storage it is given, and decodes into a value that its slot owns]).

*Two operations, two actors.*
The one write is the sender's or the wire's, made through the element.
The one copy at a hand-off is the binding's: a separate operation, which a handoff binding declares and the route's report shows as handed off.
Neither is counted as the other, and the element adds no third.

*No staging.*
The element's own code *MUST NOT* stage a payload.
It builds none in a buffer or on the heap first, and it makes no copy of its own on the way out.

*Receiving.*
The element *MUST* read a payload from the storage in which its frame arrived:

* a native payload through a view of that storage, or from the one copy that the copy route takes (xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader]);
* a serialized payload by decoding from that storage, or from the copy route's copy, into the value's slot.

The element makes no other copy of a received payload.

*A carrier.*
What a carrier may copy on a hop is stated with the carrier (xref:../up-l2/streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-byte-identity[Carriage keeps the bytes]).
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-payload-written-once~1+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the element
Verification:: test, heap-window, review
* *test*
** On a lending test double: the address that the payload is written at is the loan's, for a native route and for a serialized one.
** On a handoff test double: one copy for each frame, counted by the double, in each direction.
** On a receive route: the address that a view or a decoder reads is the address at which the frame arrived, and on the copy route it is the one copy.
* *heap-window*: sending and receiving on a native route, and on a serialized route whose class is none.
* *review*: no buffer between a sender or a wire and the storage that carries the frame, and none between an arrived frame and its reader.
Status:: Draft

*Rationale.*
A copy of a payload costs time in proportion to its size, and a staged one also needs a place to stand.
On a route that sends large values at a high rate that cost decides whether the route meets its deadline.

A staged copy is invisible to a test of values: the same bytes arrive.
So the rule is stated as a duty, and checked by where the payload is written and read.

_One rule for both kinds of route._
A native value is initialized in the storage that carries it, and a serialized value is encoded into it.
In both cases the first write of the payload is the only one.

_A handoff binding._
A binding that cannot lend needs one copy, at the hand-off.
That copy is the binding's, it is declared, and the route's report says handed off.
The element adds none of its own before or after it.

[[req-ssr-payload-route-form]]
== A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-payload-route-form~1",oft-title="A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore"]
--
*What is declared.*
A route is declared with its wire, the bound and the alignment of its payload, its heap class, its QoS and, where it needs it, a requirement to lend.
A binding declares its capability: whether it lends, the payload bound and the alignment that it carries, the counts and the QoS settings that it serves, and its own heap class (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-qos-capability[A binding honors or refuses each declared setting]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-declared[The heap class is declared, with no default]).

*Admission.*
The element *MUST* admit a declared route wherever both of these hold (xref:../seooc-up-rust/runtime-modes.adoc#req-ssr-mode-entry-admission[Every route is admitted before runtime mode]):

* the binding's declared capability serves everything that the route declares;
* every other setup check that an item states for the route passes.
Among them are its envelope, its capacity and the placing of its storage, its heap class and each guarantee that it requires, its integrity levels and, for a native route, its agreement.

*Refusal, with its reason named.*
A route that is not admitted is refused at setup, and the refusal names the route and what is missing.

* A binding refuses, in route preparation, a route that needs a capability that the binding does not have, and it names that capability.
That refusal is the binding's duty (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-qos-capability[A binding honors or refuses each declared setting]), and the element *MUST* report it with the capability that it names.
* The element *MUST* refuse a route that fails a setup check of its own, naming the check.

Every reason is a declared one: a difference between what a route declares and what its binding declares, or a setup check that an item states.
The reasons include a payload bound or an alignment that the binding does not carry, a QoS setting or a count that it does not serve, lending that the route requires and the binding cannot give, a heap class or a guarantee that is not admitted, an integrity level that does not allow the form, and a native agreement that is not established.

*What refuses nothing.*
The element *MUST NOT* admit or refuse a route because of the name of a wire, of a payload encoding or of a middleware.
It *MUST NOT* refuse a serialized route because its wire declares allocation, or because its decoded values own heap memory, where the route declares that allocation (xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-guarantees[A stronger resource guarantee is required by name]).

*A route's form.*
A route's form is three things, each fixed at setup:

* native or serialized, which its wire decides;
* lent or handed off, which its binding decides;
* on a receive route of a lending binding, a read of the shared storage or of the copy route's copy, which the levels of its writer and its reader decide (xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader]).

*Requiring lending.*
The element *MUST* refuse at setup, by name, a route whose declaration requires lending on a binding that declares that it cannot lend.
A route that does not require it runs handed off there, and its report says so.

*Fixed for the run.*
No operation in runtime mode changes a route's form: not from native to serialized, not from lent to handed off, and not from a view to a copy.
Where a route cannot have the form that it declares, setup *MUST* refuse it, and nothing serves it in another form (xref:../seooc-up-rust/runtime-modes.adoc#req-ssr-mode-entry-no-latent-refusal[Nothing is refused at runtime for a reason setup already knew]).

*Reported.*
Each route's report states its form, and each refusal states its reason (xref:../seooc-up-rust/errors.adoc#req-ssr-error-route-report[Each route reports what it got]).
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-payload-route-form~1+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the element
Verification:: test, review
* *test*
** The native wire and a serialized wire, each on a lending test double and on a handoff test double: four routes, each admitted and each reported in its own form.
** The same two wires on each real binding that the element's evidence covers.
** A route that requires lending, on a handoff binding: refused at setup by name.
** A route that a binding cannot serve: the refusal names the capability, and no route in another form is prepared.
** A serialized route whose wire declares allocation, which the route declares too: admitted on a lending test double and on a handoff test double.
** The same route with a payload bound above the binding's: refused, with the bound named.
** A native route whose alignment the binding does not carry: refused in route preparation, with the alignment named.
** A binding double that is renamed, and a wire double that is renamed, with no change in what setup admits.
* *review*: no branch of setup or of a runtime path on the name of a wire, of an encoding or of a middleware.
Status:: Draft

*Rationale.*
A message's wire is chosen where the message is declared, and a route's transport is chosen in a deployment.
An application that moved from one shared-memory middleware to another, or from shared memory to a network, would otherwise have to change its messages.

_By capability._
A binding differs from another in what it can serve: whether it lends, how large a payload it holds, which QoS it offers.
Those are what a route asks for, so those are what setup compares.
A refusal that names the missing capability tells a deployment what to change.

_Who admits, and who refuses._
The element admits, because only the element sees every check of a route.
A binding refuses for its own capability, because only the binding knows what its middleware carries.
Each names its reason, so that a deployment sees which declaration to change.
A route that owns heap memory under a class that it declares has declared nothing that a binding lacks, so that is no reason.

_Fixed for the run._
A route that changed its form in runtime mode would change its cost and its failure behaviour where no review looked.
A fallback keeps values arriving and hides the difference from every test of values.
So the form is settled at setup, reported, and left alone.

[[req-ssr-wire-encoding-checked]]
== A wire is given only a payload of its own encoding

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-wire-encoding-checked~1",oft-title="A wire is given only a payload of its own encoding",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore"]
--
*Before a wire reads.*
The element *MUST* give a received payload only to a wire of the route whose declared payload encoding identifier equals the frame's.
That binds a serialized wire and the native wire alike.

* Where no wire of the route declares the frame's identifier, the element *MUST* refuse the frame and count it once, under a cause that names the mismatch (xref:../seooc-up-rust/errors.adoc#req-ssr-error-no-silent-loss[No silent loss, and each loss counted once]).
The bytes reach no wire.
* The element *MUST NOT* take a payload's encoding from its bytes, and *MUST NOT* try one wire after another.
* The identifiers are compared as numbers, and `0` is compared as any other.
A frame that carries `0` reaches only a wire that declares `0`, for an encoding that an applicable contract defines, and the element maps `0` to no other wire.
* On an RPC server the refusal is answered as xref:../up-l2/interaction.adoc#req-ssr-peer-rpc-undecodable-answered[An undecodable request is answered] states.

*Present, empty and absent.*

* A present payload of length zero is a payload.
The element gives the wire the empty input, and the wire's format decides what it is (xref:wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-no-panic[Encoding and decoding never panic]).
* A message with no payload is no input for a wire.
The element *MUST NOT* decode an empty input in its place, and *MUST NOT* deliver a value that it made up.
* A value whose encoding has no bytes is sent as a present payload of length zero, with its identifier.

*On sending.*
A message whose payload a wire wrote carries the identifier that the wire declares.
A payload and its identifier are both present or both absent (xref:wires.adoc#encoding-identifier[the encoding identifier is the registry's]).

*Reading, and nothing else.*
This item binds a component that reads a payload as a typed value.
It gives a component that only carries a payload no reason to refuse one, and it is not a carrier's encoding filter (xref:../up-l2/streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-byte-identity[Carriage keeps the bytes]).
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-wire-encoding-checked~1+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the element
Verification:: test, fault-injection, review
* *test*
** A frame whose identifier no wire of the route declares: refused and counted under its own cause, with a wire double that records that it was not called.
** A route with two wires: each frame reaches the wire of its identifier, and neither wire is tried for the other's.
** A frame whose identifier is `0`: refused on a route whose wire declares another identifier, and given to the wire on a route whose wire declares `0`.
** A present payload of length zero: the wire is called with the empty input.
** A message with no payload: no wire is called, and no value is delivered.
** A value that encodes to no bytes: sent with a present payload of length zero and its identifier.
** An RPC request with an identifier that the server's route does not declare: answered.
** The same frames on a carrier route with no filter: forwarded unchanged.
* *fault-injection*: frames with arbitrary identifiers, with no panic and each one refused or decoded by the right wire.
* *review*: no path on which a payload's bytes reach a wire before the comparison.
Status:: Draft

*Rationale.*
A wire decodes bytes of one format.
Bytes of another format can still decode without an error, as some other value.
The identifier is the only thing in a message that says which format its payload has, and the protocol makes a reader determine the encoding first (xref:../../basics/payload_encoding_registry.adoc#acceptance[the payload encoding registry, "Carrying a Payload versus Reading It"]).
So the element compares it before any wire reads.

_Present, empty and absent._
The protocol keeps a present payload of length zero apart from an absent one (xref:../../basics/umessage.adoc[UMessage]).
Some formats give the empty input a meaning, and a decoder that was handed an empty input for a message that had no payload would return that value as if it had been sent.
So an absent payload never becomes an empty input.
What a typed role then tells its application is not decided (xref:serialized-wires.adoc#open[what is not decided for serialized wires]).

_Reading only._
A carrier forwards a payload that it cannot read, whatever its identifier.
This item is about the point at which bytes become a typed value, and it leaves carriage as it is (<<carriage>>).

[[independent]]
== A wire and a transport are chosen apart

The native wire belongs to no middleware, and Protobuf belongs to no transport.
A message's wire and a route's transport are two choices, and <<req-ssr-payload-route-form,A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name>> keeps them apart.

[cols="2,5,5",options="header"]
|===
| | On a lending binding, such as LoLa or iceoryx2 | On a handoff binding

| The native wire
| The sender initializes the value in a loan. The receiver reads it through a view of the frame, or from the copy route's copy where the levels ask for one. There is no other copy
| The sender initializes the value in storage that the route reserved at setup, and the binding copies it once at the hand-off. The route reports handed off, and is refused where it requires lending

| A serialized wire, of which Protobuf is one
| The wire encodes once into a loan. The receiving wire decodes from the frame into a value that its slot owns
| The wire encodes once into storage that the route reserved at setup, and the binding copies it once at the hand-off. The receiving wire decodes into a value that its slot owns

| No wire: the payload is only carried
| Bytes and identifier are kept, with at most one payload copy on a hop, and no reader is needed
| The same
|===

* What a lending binding owes is xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-lent-storage[Lent storage is exclusive, disjoint and stable for as long as it is held], whatever the wire.
* What the LoLa binding adds is on xref:lola.adoc[the LoLa page], and what the iceoryx2 binding adds is on xref:iceoryx2.adoc[the iceoryx2 page].
Neither page ties a wire to its binding.
* Evidence is separate for each combination: a wire that passes on one binding has shown nothing on another (xref:deployment.adoc#aou-seooc-qualified-bindings-and-wires[Bindings and wires pass their contracts]).

[[admission]]
== Admitted, or refused for a named reason

<<req-ssr-payload-route-form,A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name>> says who admits a route and who refuses one.
The examples show it for both kinds of route.
The table adds no duty.

[cols="4,3,3,4",options="header"]
|===
| A declared route | Who acts, and when | What is compared | Outcome, and what is reported

| A native route whose type the binding's storage holds, at its alignment
| The binding prepares it. The element admits it at the transition
| The type's size and alignment with the binding's bound and alignment, and every other setup check
| Admitted. The report says lent or handed off, and view or copy

| A native route whose alignment the binding does not carry
| The binding, in route preparation
| The route's alignment with the binding's
| Refused, with the alignment named. No serialized or copied route is prepared in its place (xref:native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-in-place[A native payload is written and read where it is carried, or its route is refused])

| A native route whose type the deployment has not agreed
| The element, at setup
| The type with the deployment's agreement
| Refused, with the route and the type named (xref:native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-agreement[A native type is read only under its exactly agreed layout, apart from the encoding identifier])

| A serialized route whose wire declares allocation, which the route declares too
| The binding prepares it. The element admits it at the transition
| The route's bound and QoS with the binding's, and the wire's declared class with the route's
| Admitted. The report shows the class and who allocates. Allocation that is declared is no reason to refuse (xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-guarantees[A stronger resource guarantee is required by name])

| A serialized route whose wire declares allocation that the route does not declare
| The element, at the transition
| The wire's declared class with the route's
| Refused, with the route and the undeclared allocation named (xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-declared[The heap class is declared, with no default])

| A serialized route whose payload bound is above what the binding carries
| The binding, in route preparation
| The route's bound with the binding's
| Refused, with the bound named (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-qos-capability[A binding honors or refuses each declared setting])

| A route with a QoS setting or a count that the binding does not serve
| The binding, in route preparation
| The setting with the binding's declared capability
| Refused, with the setting named. Nothing is put in its place (xref:../seooc-up-rust/qos.adoc#req-ssr-qos-no-downgrade[QoS is honored or refused, never weakened])

| A route that requires lending, on a binding that declares that it cannot lend
| The element, at setup
| The requirement with the binding's declared capability
| Refused, with lending named. Without the requirement the route runs handed off, and its report says so

| A route that requires a stronger guarantee which is not established for it
| The element, at setup
| The guarantee with the evidence for the route
| Refused, with the guarantee named. A route that requires none is not refused for it (xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-guarantees[A stronger resource guarantee is required by name])
|===

A refused route makes the transition of its transport fail, and the failure names the route and the reason (xref:../seooc-up-rust/runtime-modes.adoc#req-ssr-mode-entry-admission[Every route is admitted before runtime mode]).

[[paths]]
== The two kinds of route, side by side

[cols="2,4,4",options="header"]
|===
| | A native route | A serialized route

| What travels
| The value, as it lies in memory
| An encoding of the value, in one stated format

| The payload encoding identifier
| The one that the deployment's agreement names. It says that the payload is a native representation, and no more
| The one that the registry assigns to the wire's format. It says the format, and no more

| What agrees the type
| The deployment's agreement on the type's complete layout, which the element establishes for a writer and a reader before any view (xref:native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-agreement[A native type is read only under its exactly agreed layout, apart from the encoding identifier])
| What the wire states. Many formats do not name the type of their bytes (xref:serialized-wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-serialized-format[A serialized wire reads and writes one stated format])

| Which types
| One fixed layout, with no address, no invalid bit pattern and no destructor (xref:native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-type[A native payload type holds its whole value in one fixed layout])
| Any type that the format encodes. Its values may own resources, heap memory included (xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-guarantees[A stronger resource guarantee is required by name])

| Size
| Exact: the type's size
| A largest encoding that the wire states, or a bound that the route declares (xref:serialized-wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-encode-decode[A serialized wire encodes once into the storage it is given, and decodes into a value that its slot owns])

| Sending
| The value is initialized once, in the storage that carries it
| The value is encoded once, into the storage that carries it

| Receiving
| A checked, read-only view that lives no longer than the frame's lease (xref:native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-in-place[A native payload is written and read where it is carried, or its route is refused])
| A value that its slot owns, which outlives the frame (xref:serialized-wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-encode-decode[A serialized wire encodes once into the storage it is given, and decodes into a value that its slot owns])

| Across integrity levels
| A view only from a writer at or above the reader. Otherwise one copy into storage that the reader owns, validated there, or a refusal at setup
| The same rule for the shared bytes: where the writer is below the reader or unknown, the frame is copied once and decoded from the copy (xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader])

| Heap
| None, on every path (xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-none[No heap in runtime mode])
| What the wire and the binding declare for each direction: none, or allocation (xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-declared[The heap class is declared, with no default])

| Stronger guarantees
| None to require: a native route runs no destructor and uses no heap
| Each one only where the route requires it by name and it is established (xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-guarantees[A stronger resource guarantee is required by name])

| A bad input
| A frame of the wrong shape, or from a writer whose layout identity is not established to be the reader's, is refused at admission. Wrong contents of the right shape are a wrong value, never undefined behaviour
| An input that is not an encoding is a returned error, never a panic (xref:wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-no-panic[Encoding and decoding never panic])

| A route that cannot be served
| Refused at setup, with the missing capability named. Nothing serves it as a serialized or a copied route
| Refused at setup, with the missing capability named. Allocation that the route declares is no such reason. Nothing serves it as a native route

| Carried by a Streamer
| Under the rules for a native representation: the same ABI on both sides, at most one payload copy on a hop, and a validated copy where a route crosses upward (xref:peers.adoc#req-ssr-native-abi-agreed[A native representation never crosses an ABI]; xref:../up-l2/streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-byte-identity[Carriage keeps the bytes]; xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-carrier-crossing[Upward carrier crossings copy once, or are refused])
| Bytes and identifier kept, with no reader (xref:../up-l2/streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-byte-identity[Carriage keeps the bytes])
|===

[[lifetime]]
== Who owns what, and until when

One rule holds on both kinds of route.

* *One end.*
A lease ends once, by the transition that ends it: a send, a give-back, a return or a destruction.
* *Or none.*
Safe code can forget a lease, and then nothing ends it.
What the lease holds stays charged for the life of the process, and it is never reused, given a second owner, returned or destroyed.

No row of the table asks for an end that a forgotten lease cannot have.

[cols="3,4,4,3",options="header"]
|===
| What | On a native route | On a serialized route | Rule

| The value at the sender
| It exists only in the storage that carries it: the sender writes it there
| It is the application's. The wire borrows it to encode, and leaves it as it was
| xref:native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-in-place[A native payload is written and read where it is carried, or its route is refused]; xref:serialized-wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-encode-decode[A serialized wire encodes once into the storage it is given, and decodes into a value that its slot owns]

| The storage before it is sent
| The sender's lease holds it, alone. A send consumes it, or the drop of the lease gives it back, exactly once. No pending operation holds it
| The same
| xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-lent-storage[Lent storage is exclusive, disjoint and stable for as long as it is held]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/release.adoc#req-ssr-release-lease-drop[A lease gives back its slot and nothing else]; xref:../seooc-up-rust/build.adoc#req-ssr-async-seam[The asynchronous interface has a written, executor-independent contract]

| The frame once it is sent
| The transport's. The sender never writes it again
| The same
| xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-lent-storage[Lent storage is exclusive, disjoint and stable for as long as it is held]; xref:deployment.adoc#aou-seooc-peer-access[Only authorized processes reach the transport, and peers are faulty, never malicious]

| The frame at the receiver
| The receiver's lease holds it for as long as a view of it lives
| The element holds it while the wire decodes. It can go back as soon as that is done
| xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-frame-drop[What a received frame's destructor may do]

| What the application reads
| A view. It borrows the frame, and it ends before the frame is returned
| A value. Its slot owns it, then the application's lease, and it borrows nothing
| xref:native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-in-place[A native payload is written and read where it is carried, or its route is refused]; xref:serialized-wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-encode-decode[A serialized wire encodes once into the storage it is given, and decodes into a value that its slot owns]

| The end, when the lease is dropped
| The frame is returned, exactly once. No destructor runs, and no heap is freed
| The value is destroyed, exactly once, on a path that the route's class and guarantees allow, and its slot goes back

[[carriage]]
== Carrying a payload is not reading it

* *Reading.*
A component that turns a payload into a typed value first compares its encoding identifier with a wire's, and refuses what no wire of the route declares (<<req-ssr-wire-encoding-checked,A wire is given only a payload of its own encoding>>).
* *Carrying.*
A carrier keeps a serialized payload's bytes and its identifier, needs no decoder, and refuses no payload because its identifier is reserved, unassigned, private or unknown to it.
The rule is xref:../up-l2/streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-byte-identity[Carriage keeps the bytes], and it binds whom that item binds: this page widens it to no other component.
* *A filter is not a decoder.*
A route's encoding filter is a stated policy of a carrier.
It exists only where deployment data states one, and a refusal under it is counted as that policy's (xref:../up-l2/streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-qos-per-leg[Both legs are checked, at setup and per frame]).
Which wires a typed reader has says what that reader can read.
It changes nothing about what a carrier forwards, and a filter changes nothing about what a reader can read.
* *A native representation.*
A carrier admits a frame that names a native representation under the rules for one, which are separate from the rules for serialized bytes (xref:peers.adoc#req-ssr-native-abi-agreed[A native representation never crosses an ABI]; xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-carrier-crossing[Upward carrier crossings copy once, or are refused]).

=== What this page leaves as it is

* The rules for an RPC request that cannot be decoded, and for a response, are those of xref:../up-l2/interaction.adoc[the interaction rules].
This page changes none of them.
* What a transport or an SDK that is not written against the element's contract carries, and how, is stated only in xref:../../contract-generations.adoc#transition[the transition table].
* End-to-end protection is deferred.
It is part of neither path here, and its items bind once that wire is delivered (xref:../seooc-up-rust/e2e-wire.adoc[the end-to-end protection wire]).
