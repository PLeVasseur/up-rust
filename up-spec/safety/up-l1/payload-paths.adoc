= uP-L1 safety: Payload paths, end to end
:toc: preamble

The key words "*MUST*", "*MUST NOT*", "*REQUIRED*", "*SHALL*", "*SHALL NOT*", "*SHOULD*", "*SHOULD NOT*", "*RECOMMENDED*", "*MAY*", and "*OPTIONAL*" in this document are to be interpreted as described in https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/bcp14[IETF BCP14 (RFC2119 & RFC8174)]

Conventions and tags: xref:../README.adoc[the safety area's README].
This layer, and whom its items bind: xref:README.adoc[safety at uP-L1].
The two kinds of wire: xref:native-wire.adoc[the native wire] and xref:serialized-wires.adoc[serialized wires, and the values they own].
The duties of every binding: xref:bindings.adoc[allocated to each binding].

NOTE: A payload reaches its reader on one of two kinds of route, native or serialized, over one of two kinds of binding, lending or handoff.
The choice of a wire and the choice of a transport are separate, so all four combinations exist.
A component that only carries a payload is a third case, and it reads nothing.

This page holds:

* the three requirements of this page: <<req-ssr-payload-written-once,A payload is written once, into the storage that carries it>>; <<req-ssr-payload-route-form,A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name>>; and <<req-ssr-wire-encoding-checked,A wire is given only a payload of its own encoding>>;
* the combinations of wire and transport (<<independent>>);
* who admits a route and who refuses one, with examples (<<admission>>);
* the two kinds of route side by side (<<paths>>);
* who owns what, and until when (<<lifetime>>);
* carrying and reading a payload (<<carriage>>).

Each item's statement says whom it binds (xref:../README.adoc#req-ssr-requirements-bind[A requirement binds the actors to which it gives a duty]).

[[req-ssr-payload-written-once]]
== A payload is written once, into the storage that carries it

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-payload-written-once~3",oft-title="A payload is written once, into the storage that carries it",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore"]
--
*Sending.*
The element *MUST* have a payload written exactly once, directly into the storage that carries its frame:

* on a lending binding, the loan (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-lent-storage[Lent storage is exclusive, disjoint and stable for as long as it is held]);
* on a handoff binding, storage that the route reserved at setup.

That holds for a native payload and for a serialized payload.
The one write is made through the element, by the sender of a native payload or by the wire of a serialized one, as xref:native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-in-place[A native payload is written and read where it is carried, or its route is refused] and xref:serialized-wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-encode-decode[A serialized wire encodes once into the storage it is given, and decodes into a value that its slot owns] state.

*No staging.*
The element's own code *MUST NOT* stage a payload.
It builds none in a buffer or on the heap first, and it makes no copy of its own on the way out.

*The copy at a hand-off.*
At each hand-off, on sending and on receiving, a handoff binding *MUST* copy the payload exactly once, as an operation of its own that it declares with its capability (<<req-ssr-payload-route-form,A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name>>), and *MUST NOT* make any other copy of the payload for that hand-off.
The route's report shows such a route as handed off (xref:../seooc-up-rust/errors.adoc#req-ssr-error-route-report[Each route reports what it got]).

*Before it is sent.*
On a serialized route, the element *MUST* hold the storage that a payload is written into through one owner alone, from the moment it gives that storage out until the payload is sent or the storage is given back.
The element *MUST* have a send consume that owner, so that nothing writes the storage through it afterwards, and *MUST* have the drop of an unsent owner give the storage back exactly once (xref:../seooc-up-rust/release.adoc#req-ssr-release-lease-drop[A lease gives back its slot and nothing else]).
Where safe code forgets that owner, the element *MUST* keep the storage charged to its route for the life of the process, and *MUST NOT* reuse it, return it or give it to another owner.
That no pending operation holds a transmit loan, on a serialized route as on a native one, is stated in xref:../seooc-up-rust/build.adoc#req-ssr-async-seam[The asynchronous interface has a written, executor-independent contract].
What holds for a native payload's storage before it is sent is stated in xref:native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-in-place[A native payload is written and read where it is carried, or its route is refused], and what a lending binding keeps for the storage that it lends in xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-lent-storage[Lent storage is exclusive, disjoint and stable for as long as it is held].

*Receiving.*
The element *MUST* read a payload from the storage in which its frame arrived:

* a native payload through a view of that storage, or from the one copy that the copy route takes (xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader]);
* a serialized payload by decoding from that storage, or from the copy route's copy, into the value's slot.

*The only copies.*
The element *MUST NOT* make any other copy of a payload, sent or received.
Between a sender and a receiver on one transport, a payload is then copied only in two cases, each fixed at setup and reported (<<req-ssr-payload-route-form,A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name>>):

* at each hand-off, once, by the handoff binding;
* on the copy route, once, by the element.

For the element on a native route, this clause replaces the clause "The only copies" of revision 1 of xref:native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-in-place[A native payload is written and read where it is carried, or its route is refused], and its two cases are those of that clause.

*A carrier.*
What a carrier may copy on a hop is stated with the carrier (xref:../up-l2/streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-byte-identity[Carriage keeps the bytes]; xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-carrier-crossing[Upward carrier crossings copy once, or are refused]).
--

* *test*
** On a lending test double: the address that the payload is written at is the loan's, for a native route and for a serialized one.
** On a handoff test double: one copy for each frame, counted by the double, in each direction, and no other copy.
** On a receive route: the address that a view or a decoder reads is the address at which the frame arrived, and on the copy route it is the one copy.
** On a serialized route, on a lending test double and on a handoff test double: a send consumes the storage's owner; an unsent owner's drop gives the storage back once, counted by the double; and a forgotten owner's storage stays charged at the end of the run and is never given out again.
* *compile-fail* or *test*: on a serialized route, an attempted write through the storage's owner after a send, refused before any access to the storage: by the compiler, or at run time where the attempt compiles.
* *heap-window*: sending and receiving on a native route, and on a serialized route whose class is none.
* *review*: no buffer between a sender or a wire and the storage that carries the frame, and none between an arrived frame and its reader.

*Rationale.*
A copy of a payload costs time in proportion to its size, and a staged one also needs a place to stand.
On a route that sends large values at a high rate that cost decides whether the route meets its deadline.

A staged copy is invisible to a test of values: the same bytes arrive.
So the rule fixes where the payload is written and read, and is checked there.

_A handoff binding._
A binding that cannot lend needs one copy, at the hand-off.

_Before it is sent._
A loan that a pending operation held would stay out of its route's storage for as long as the operation stayed pending.
That holds for a loan that a wire encodes into as for one that a sender writes.

[[req-ssr-payload-route-form]]
== A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-payload-route-form~2",oft-title="A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore,SafetyBinding"]
--
*What is declared.*
A route is declared with its wire, the bound and the alignment of its payload, its heap class, its QoS and, where it needs it, a requirement to lend.
The smallest payload of a route is its type's size on a native route.
On a serialized route it is, for sending, the smallest encoding that the route's wire states (xref:serialized-wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-encode-decode[A serialized wire encodes once into the storage it is given, and decodes into a value that its slot owns], "Sizing"), or zero where the wire states none, and, for receiving, zero.
A binding's capability is what it declares of whether it lends, the payload bound and the alignment that it carries, the smallest payload that it carries where it carries no shorter one, and the counts and the QoS settings that it serves: the binding declares it as xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-qos-capability[A binding honors or refuses each declared setting] states, and its own heap class as xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-declared[The heap class is declared, with no default] states.

*Admission.*
The element *MUST* admit a declared route wherever both of these hold (xref:../seooc-up-rust/runtime-modes.adoc#req-ssr-mode-entry-admission[Every route is admitted before runtime mode]):

* the binding's declared capability serves everything that the route declares, in each direction that the route uses from the route's smallest payload in that direction to its bound;
* every other setup check that an item states for the route passes.
Among them are its envelope, its capacity and the placing of its storage, its heap class and each guarantee that it requires, its integrity levels and, for a native route, its agreement.

*Refusal, with its reason named.*
The element *MUST* refuse at setup a route that it does not admit, and *MUST* name the route and what is missing in that refusal.

* A binding refuses, in route preparation, a route that needs a capability that the binding does not have, as xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-qos-capability[A binding honors or refuses each declared setting] states, and it *MUST* name the route and that capability in that refusal.
The element *MUST* report that refusal with the capability that it names.
* The element *MUST* refuse a route that fails a setup check of its own, naming the check.

*The reasons.*
The element and each binding *MUST NOT* refuse a declared route for a reason that is not a declared one: a difference between what the route declares and what its binding declares, or a setup check that an item states.
The reasons include a payload bound, a smallest payload or an alignment that the binding does not carry, a QoS setting or a count that it does not serve, lending that the route requires and the binding cannot give, a heap class or a guarantee that is not admitted, an integrity level that does not allow the form, and a native agreement that is not established.
A setup operation that fails for another cause, such as a resource that the platform does not grant, is no refusal of the route's declaration: it is returned as the named error that xref:../seooc-up-rust/runtime-modes.adoc#req-ssr-mode-init-failure[Initialization failure] states.

*Names and declared allocation.*
The element *MUST NOT* admit or refuse a route, and a binding *MUST NOT* refuse one, because of the name of a wire or of a payload encoding.
The element *MUST NOT* admit or refuse a route because of the name of a middleware either.
The element *MUST NOT* refuse a serialized route because its wire declares allocation that the route declares, except a route that needs the copy route, as xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader], "Reporting and heap", states; that it refuses no such route because its decoded values own heap memory is stated in xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-guarantees[A stronger resource guarantee is required by name, and admitted or refused].

*A route's form.*
A route's form is three things, each fixed at setup:

* native or serialized, which its wire decides;
* lent or handed off, which its binding decides;
* on a receive route of a lending binding, a read of the shared storage or of the copy route's copy, as xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader] decides.

For a composition, as xref:wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-scope[What a wire is, and the wire contract], "More than one wire", defines it, the element *MUST* fix the form of each admitted wire at setup.

*Requiring lending.*
The element *MUST* refuse at setup, by name, a route whose declaration requires lending on a binding that declares that it cannot lend.
A route that does not require it runs handed off there, and its report says so.
A lending binding's own refusal to lend for a route is stated in xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-lent-storage[Lent storage is exclusive, disjoint and stable for as long as it is held].

*Fixed for the run.*
The element *MUST NOT* let an operation in runtime mode change a route's form, for example from native to serialized, from lent to handed off or from a view to a copy.
A binding *MUST NOT* change in runtime mode whether it lends or hands off for a route.
Where a route cannot have the form that it declares, the element *MUST* refuse it at setup, and *MUST NOT* serve it in another form (xref:../seooc-up-rust/runtime-modes.adoc#req-ssr-mode-entry-no-latent-refusal[Nothing is refused at runtime for a reason setup already knew]).
The agreement under which a native route's type is read is fixed with the form (xref:native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-agreement[A native type is read only under its exactly agreed layout]).

*Reported.*
What the element states of each route's form in its report is specified in xref:../seooc-up-rust/errors.adoc#req-ssr-error-route-report[Each route reports what it got].

*Rationale.*
A message's wire is chosen where the message is declared, and a route's transport is chosen in a deployment.
An application that moved from one shared-memory middleware to another, or from shared memory to a network, would otherwise have to change its messages.

_By capability._
A binding differs from another in what it can serve: whether it lends, how large a payload it holds, which QoS it offers.
A route asks for these, so setup compares them.
A route that receives meets the input of every compatible writer, not only what its own wire encodes, so its smallest payload is zero.

_Who admits, and who refuses._
The element admits, because only the element sees every check of a route.
A binding refuses for its own capability, because only the binding knows what its middleware carries.
Each names its reason, so that a deployment sees which declaration to change.
A route that owns heap memory under a class that it declares has declared nothing that a binding lacks, so that is no reason.

_Fixed for the run._
A route that changed its form in runtime mode would change its cost and its failure behaviour where no review looked.
A fallback keeps values arriving and hides the difference from every test of values.
So the form is fixed at setup and reported.

[[req-ssr-wire-encoding-checked]]
== A wire is given only a payload of its own encoding

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-wire-encoding-checked~2",oft-title="A wire is given only a payload of its own encoding",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore"]
--
*Before a wire reads.*
The element *MUST* give a received payload only to a wire of the route whose declared payload encoding identifier equals the frame's.
That holds alike on a route with a serialized wire and on a route with the native wire.
The element *MUST* make that comparison before it gives the payload to any wire of the route to decode or to verify, the end-to-end protection wire included.
A wire that decodes the frame's metadata (xref:wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-metadata-format[A wire states the format of its frame metadata, and decodes it]) does not read the payload by doing so.
A frame that a check made before this comparison refuses, such as the TTL check of xref:../seooc-up-rust/qos.adoc#req-ssr-qos-ttl[Expired messages are not processed], gets neither this comparison nor a check of the presence of its payload, and its one count is that check's (xref:../seooc-up-rust/errors.adoc#req-ssr-error-no-silent-loss[No silent loss, and each loss counted once]).

* Where no wire of the route declares the frame's identifier, the element *MUST* refuse the frame and count it once, under a cause that names the mismatch (xref:../seooc-up-rust/errors.adoc#req-ssr-error-no-silent-loss[No silent loss, and each loss counted once]).
* The element *MUST NOT* take a payload's encoding from its bytes, and *MUST NOT* try one wire after another.
* The element *MUST* compare the identifiers as numbers, and `0` as any other.
A frame that carries `0` reaches only a wire that declares `0`, for an encoding that an applicable contract defines.
* On an RPC server, whether and how the refusal of a request under this item is answered, and where this comparison stands among the checks of a request, are stated in xref:../up-l2/interaction.adoc#req-ssr-peer-rpc-undecodable-answered[An undecodable request is answered].

*Present, empty and absent.*

* A present payload of length zero is a payload.
The element *MUST* give the wire the empty input, and the wire's format decides what it is (xref:wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-no-panic[Encoding and decoding never panic]).
* A message with no payload is no input for a wire.
The element *MUST NOT* decode an empty input in its place, and *MUST NOT* deliver a value that it made up.
* Where a typed operation requires a value and its message has no payload, the element *MUST* refuse the message with the result `+missing_payload+`, which says that the payload is absent and is no decoding error.
The element *MUST* return that result where the operation returns a result to its caller, and otherwise *MUST* count the refusal once under the cause `+missing_payload+` (xref:../seooc-up-rust/errors.adoc#req-ssr-error-no-silent-loss[No silent loss, and each loss counted once]).
Where the declared contract of an operation permits a message with no payload, the element *MUST* report the absence as that contract states.
* The element *MUST* send a value whose encoding has no bytes as a present payload of length zero, with its identifier.

*A response to an RPC call.*
For a response tied to an RPC call, the operation is the call, and this item's refusal of the response neither ends the call nor itself returns its result to the call's caller.
The refused response completes nothing, leaves the call open and is counted once under the cause of its refusal, as xref:../up-l2/interaction.adoc#req-ssr-rpc-check-at-arrival[A response completes its call only once its wire accepts it] states.
What the call names where it ends at its TTL, and the responses for which that item replaces this item's refusal, result and count, are stated in that item.

*A message on an end-to-end protected receive route.*
On a receive route that selects the end-to-end protection wire, whether an RPC client's response route, an RPC server's request route or a topic's or a notification's receive route, the refusal, the result and the count of a message that carries no payload of that wire's encoding, a message with no payload included, are stated in xref:../seooc-up-rust/e2e-wire.adoc#req-ssr-e2e-refusal-counted[End-to-end refusals are named, and counted once].
For that message, those rules replace this item's refusal, result and count for an identifier that no wire declares and for a missing payload, its rule that gives a present payload of length zero to the wire of its identifier, and its report of an absence that an operation's declared contract permits.
This item's rules that keep a payload from a wire of another encoding, and an absent payload from becoming an empty input, still apply to it.

*On sending.*
The element *MUST* give a message whose payload a wire wrote the identifier that the wire declares.
A payload and its identifier are both present or both absent (xref:wires.adoc#encoding-identifier[the encoding identifier is the registry's]).

*What the identifier says.*
An equal identifier says which format a payload has, and no more.
It identifies no type, and neither does a decoding that succeeds.

*Typed sending and receiving.*
This item applies where a payload is written or read as a typed value, on a typed send and on a typed receive.
It does not apply where a payload is only carried (xref:../up-l2/streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-byte-identity[Carriage keeps the bytes]).

** A message whose payload a wire wrote: sent with the identifier that the wire declares.
** A frame on a route that only carries it: no wire is given its payload under this item (carriage is checked under xref:../up-l2/streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-byte-identity[Carriage keeps the bytes]).
* *fault-injection*: frames with arbitrary identifiers, with no panic and each one refused or decoded by the right wire, or, for a tied response whose commstatus is not `+Ok+`, given to no other wire.
* *review*: no path on which a payload's bytes reach a wire before the comparison.

*Rationale.*
A wire decodes bytes of one format.
Bytes of another format can still decode without an error, as some other value.
The identifier is the only thing in a message that says which format its payload has, and the protocol makes a reader determine the encoding first (xref:../../basics/payload_encoding_registry.adoc#acceptance[the payload encoding registry, "Carrying a Payload versus Reading It"]).
So the element compares it before any wire reads.

_Present, empty and absent._
The protocol keeps a present payload of length zero apart from an absent one (xref:../../basics/umessage.adoc[UMessage]).
Some formats give the empty input a meaning, and a decoder that was handed an empty input for a message that had no payload would return that value as if it had been sent.
So an absent payload never becomes an empty input.
A typed operation that requires a value has none to give for such a message, so it refuses the message by a result that names the cause.

_Typed sending and receiving._
A carrier forwards a serialized payload without reading it, as xref:../up-l2/streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-byte-identity[Carriage keeps the bytes] states.

[[independent]]
== A wire and a transport are chosen apart

NOTE: A message's wire, such as the native wire or Protobuf, and a route's transport are two choices, and <<req-ssr-payload-route-form,A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name>> keeps them apart.

* The native wire, on either kind of binding: xref:native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-in-place[A native payload is written and read where it is carried, or its route is refused].
* A serialized wire, on either kind of binding: xref:serialized-wires.adoc#req-ssr-wire-encode-decode[A serialized wire encodes once into the storage it is given, and decodes into a value that its slot owns].
* A payload that is only carried, with no wire: xref:../up-l2/streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-byte-identity[Carriage keeps the bytes].

[[admission]]
== Admitted, or refused for a named reason

<<req-ssr-payload-route-form,A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name>> says who admits a route and who refuses one.
The examples show it for both kinds of route.

.Declared routes, and what setup does with each
====
In each admitted case, every other capability and setup check of the route passes, and every setup operation succeeds.
In each refused case, the condition named is the one that fails.

[cols="4,3,3,4",options="header"]
|===
| A declared route | Who acts, and when | What is compared | Outcome, and what is reported

| A native route whose type the binding's storage holds, at its alignment
| The binding prepares it. The element admits it at the transition
| The type's size and alignment with the binding's bound and alignment, and every other setup check
| Admitted. The report states the route's form

| A native route whose alignment the binding does not carry
| The binding, in route preparation
| The route's alignment with the binding's
| Refused, with the alignment named. No serialized or copied route is prepared in its place (xref:native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-in-place[A native payload is written and read where it is carried, or its route is refused])

| A native route whose type the deployment has not agreed
| The element, at setup
| The type with the deployment's agreement
| Refused, with the route and the type named (xref:native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-agreement[A native type is read only under its exactly agreed layout])

| A serialized route whose wire declares allocation, which the route declares too
| The binding prepares it. The element admits it at the transition
| The route's bound and QoS with the binding's, and the wire's declared class with the route's
| Admitted. The report shows the class and who allocates (xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-guarantees[A stronger resource guarantee is required by name, and admitted or refused])

| A serialized route whose wire declares allocation that the route does not declare
| The element, at the transition
| The wire's declared class with the route's
| Refused, with the route and the undeclared allocation named (xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-declared[The heap class is declared, with no default])

| A serialized route whose payload bound is above what the binding carries
| The binding, in route preparation
| The route's bound with the binding's
| Refused, with the bound named (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-qos-capability[A binding honors or refuses each declared setting])

| A serialized route that receives, or whose wire states no smallest encoding, on a binding whose declared smallest payload is above zero
| The binding, in route preparation
| The route's smallest payload with the binding's
| Refused, with the size named (xref:../seooc-up-rust/runtime-modes.adoc#req-ssr-mode-entry-no-latent-refusal[Nothing is refused at runtime for a reason setup already knew])

| A route with a QoS setting or a count that the binding does not serve
| The binding, in route preparation
| The setting with the binding's declared capability
| Refused, with the setting named. Nothing is put in its place (xref:../seooc-up-rust/qos.adoc#req-ssr-qos-no-downgrade[QoS is honored or refused, never weakened])

| A route that requires lending, on a binding that declares that it cannot lend
| The element, at setup
| The requirement with the binding's declared capability
| Refused, with lending named

| The same route, declared without that requirement
| The binding prepares it. The element admits it at the transition
| The route's declaration with the binding's declared capability
| Admitted. The report says handed off

| A route that requires a stronger guarantee which is not established for it
| The element, at setup
| The guarantee with the evidence for the route
| Refused, with the guarantee named (xref:../seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-guarantees[A stronger resource guarantee is required by name, and admitted or refused])
|===
====

What a refused route does to the transition of its transport is stated in xref:../seooc-up-rust/runtime-modes.adoc#req-ssr-mode-entry-admission[Every route is admitted before runtime mode].
