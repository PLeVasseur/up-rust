:toc: preamble

The key words "*MUST*", "*MUST NOT*", "*REQUIRED*", "*SHALL*", "*SHALL NOT*", "*SHOULD*", "*SHOULD NOT*", "*RECOMMENDED*", "*MAY*", and "*OPTIONAL*" in this document are to be interpreted as described in https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/bcp14[IETF BCP14 (RFC2119 & RFC8174)]

Conventions, tags and status words: xref:../README.adoc[the safety area's README].
This layer, and whom it binds: xref:README.adoc[safety at uP-L1].
The duties of every binding, which the iceoryx2 binding meets too: xref:bindings.adoc[allocated to each binding].

This page is the safety counterpart of xref:../../up-l1/iceoryx2.adoc[the iceoryx2 transport chapter].
It holds what is specific to one transport:

* the two requirements allocated to the iceoryx2 binding alone (tag `SafetyIceoryx2`), which `up-transport-iceoryx2-rust` covers;
* the two assumptions of use that bind only a deployment on iceoryx2 (tag `SafetyManual`), which that binding's safety manual states;
* what the rules of every binding, of the element and of every deployment meet on iceoryx2 (<<index>>).

== Whom this page binds

* The two requirements bind the iceoryx2 binding of the up-rust SEooC, and no other transport.
* The two assumptions bind an integrator that deploys on that binding and claims the element's safety-assurance scope (xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#who[who the assumptions bind]).
They are the up-rust SEooC's assumptions, as `seooc` in their IDs says, and they are deployment duties: no requirement on the binding is met by them, and they replace none.
* Nothing here binds an iceoryx2 transport written for another SDK, or changes the protocol's iceoryx2 chapter.
That chapter names version 0.6.1 of the iceoryx2 protocol, and the items here are written against iceoryx2 0.9.
How the two relate is open (xref:../../contract-generations.adoc#transition[the transition table]).
* An entry of <<index>> binds whoever its own item binds.

The assumptions of every transport bind a deployment on iceoryx2 as well: xref:deployment.adoc[what a deployment provides for its transports].
Each "Violation" text uses the three classes of xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#violation-classes[how a violation shows up].
Where one says instead that the contract requires no check, it states a limit of the contract and asserts no class, as that section explains.

[[req-ssr-binding-depth-from-deployment]]
== iceoryx2's service depth comes from deployment data

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-binding-depth-from-deployment~1",oft-title="iceoryx2's service depth comes from deployment data",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyIceoryx2"]
--
The iceoryx2 binding *MUST* take each service's depth from deployment data, and create the service with it.

When it opens a service that already exists, it *MUST* compare that service's depth with the deployment's, and refuse a mismatch at setup, naming the route and both depths.
--

[[req-ssr-binding-iceoryx2-panic-paths]]
== iceoryx2's allowed panic paths are named, bounded and disclosed

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-binding-iceoryx2-panic-paths~1",oft-title="iceoryx2's allowed panic paths are named, bounded and disclosed",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyIceoryx2"]
--
*What this item is.*
These are the middleware panic paths that xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-no-panic[No panic on any peer input] and xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-frame-drop[What a received frame's destructor may do] allow the iceoryx2 binding, each as a named instance.
Each is disclosed as a known anomaly in the binding's README, and none is a waiver.

*The per-port mutex.*
iceoryx2's per-port mutex, whose failure on lock or on unlock reaches a `+fatal_panic!+`, is allowed only while three things stay true:

* that failure is reachable solely through a fault of the process-private lock: corrupted process memory, a recursion-count overflow, or an OS error;
* none of these is reachable through any peer input, sequence, death or restart, nor through any sequence of calls an application can make through the element, such as dropping a frame while the same thread holds the port;
* it is disclosed as a known anomaly in that binding's README (`+UPD-IOX2-0001+`, revision 2).

*The other allowed paths.*

* its two `+unwrap+`s in `+blocking_send+`, which only a failing OS clock call reaches, behind the clock check the binding makes at startup (`+UPD-IOX2-0002+`);
* two invariants that its process-private reference counts keep: that no buffer goes back to its pool while still referenced, and that none is released twice (`+UPD-IOX2-0003+`);
* a descriptor that other code in the same process closes between iceoryx2's `+socket()+` and `+fcntl+` calls, on a peer change (`+UPD-IOX2-0003+`);
* `+close()+` or `+munmap()+` failing when it releases a departed peer's descriptor or mapping, which only a descriptor closed elsewhere, a corrupted mapping or an error that Linux does not document for these calls can cause (`+UPD-IOX2-0004+`);
* a queue's push counter that never wraps: accepted on 64-bit targets, where it needs over 580 years at 10^9^ sends per second; on a 32-bit target the binding refuses a service that keeps history (`+UPD-IOX2-0005+`).

*Heap exhaustion during a peer change.*
Heap exhaustion during a peer change aborts iceoryx2's process rather than panicking it.
It is part of the deployment's heap sizing under `+UPW-iceoryx2-1+` (`+UPD-IOX2-0006+`).

*No other path.*
iceoryx2's known failures are not allowed paths: <<aou-seooc-iceoryx2-limits,A deployment on iceoryx2 accepts that binding's limits, and no known failure as one>> lists them.
Any other path needs its own explicit acceptance decision (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-no-panic[No panic on any peer input]).
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-binding-iceoryx2-panic-paths~1+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the iceoryx2 binding
Verification:: fault-injection, test, gate, review
* *fault-injection*: peer input, death and restart never reach the per-port mutex's panic path.
* *test*: three tests, each in a child process on real iceoryx2, that reproduce its index bug on a connection change, a full expired-connection buffer, and a completion-queue entry written before the first send.
* *gate*: the panic inventory re-run on every iceoryx2 version change and on every binding change to its entry points.
A new runtime panic site holds the change until it is decided.
* *review*
** The binding's lock use, for every call sequence the element allows.
** The README's known-anomaly rows.
Status:: Draft

*Rationale.*
The upstream record to track for these paths is https://github.com/eclipse-iceoryx/iceoryx2/issues/763[iceoryx2 issue #763].

Each accepted middleware anomaly needs its own bounded instance, and peer-triggered known failures cannot be reclassified as accepted anomalies.

The panic sites in iceoryx2's wait path stay known failures until the binding makes them unreachable, by a binding-owned `+poll(2)+` loop or a reviewed equivalent with an argument at source level.
The named descriptor anomaly above cannot excuse them.

These instances are one binding's, so they are stated with that binding.
The two rules of every binding allow a path only as an instance that this item names.
The binding's duties stay with the binding.

[[aou-seooc-iceoryx2-level-separation]]
== On iceoryx2, each integrity level has its own processes, domain and OS user

[.specitem,oft-sid="aou~seooc-iceoryx2-level-separation~1",oft-title="On iceoryx2, each integrity level has its own processes, domain and OS user",oft-needs="uman",oft-tags="SafetyManual"]
--
For a deployment that uses iceoryx2, the integrator provides the following.

* *The process's level.* On iceoryx2 a transport runs at its process's level.
* *One level per service.* iceoryx2 is a binding whose ports write shared state both ways, so xref:deployment.adoc#aou-seooc-peer-access[Only authorized processes reach the transport, and peers are faulty, never malicious], "Two-way shared state", binds every iceoryx2 service.
* *Domains and OS users.* Each level runs in its own iceoryx2 domain, and its processes under their own OS user, so that a faulty lower process cannot open, map or remove a higher level's shared memory.
--

*Violation:*

* A port whose level differs from its service's recorded level is *refused at setup* (xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader]).
* A peer at another level that the deployment lets onto a service can crash or corrupt its peers' memory: *undetectable* by the element (xref:deployment.adoc#aou-seooc-peer-access[Only authorized processes reach the transport, and peers are faulty, never malicious]).
* This contract requires no check of a domain or an OS user: no requirement has the element or the binding make one.
So it guarantees no detection of two levels that share a domain or an OS user, and this text gives that violation none of the three classes.
The integrator validates the separation itself, as it validates every assumption (xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc[the element's assumptions of use]), and relies on no automatic check.
This states what the contract guarantees, not what an implementation can or cannot see.

[horizontal]
ID:: `+aou~seooc-iceoryx2-level-separation~1+`
ASIL:: B
Status:: Draft
ISO 26262:: 6-7.4.8, 9-6, 6-Annex D.2.4 (part-clause numbers only)

*Rationale.*
A shared reference cannot remain valid against an uncontrolled concurrent writer, and bidirectional shared state also exposes a publisher to a faulty consumer.
So on iceoryx2 the levels are kept apart by what the OS enforces.

These duties bind only a deployment on iceoryx2, so they are stated with that binding and not with the duties of every transport.
They are duties of the integrator: this item gives the binding no requirement.

The "Violation" text says what the contract guarantees about them, and no more.
It guarantees the refusal of a port at another level than its service, which xref:integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-zero-copy[Zero-copy only from a writer at or above the reader] requires.
It guarantees no check of a domain or an OS user, because no requirement asks for one.

[[aou-seooc-iceoryx2-limits]]
== A deployment on iceoryx2 accepts that binding's limits, and no known failure as one

[.specitem,oft-sid="aou~seooc-iceoryx2-limits~1",oft-title="A deployment on iceoryx2 accepts that binding's limits, and no known failure as one",oft-needs="uman",oft-tags="SafetyManual"]
--
*Target.*
The iceoryx2 binding claims no 32-bit target, because iceoryx2 0.9 needs 64-bit atomics and its bare-metal port is a proof of concept.

*Accepted limits and known anomalies.*
The integrator accepts the limits and known anomalies that each binding's README states (xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-readme[The README says what the middleware cannot guarantee]; xref:deployment.adoc#aou-seooc-qualified-bindings-and-wires[Bindings and wires pass their contracts]). For a deployment that uses iceoryx2, these include:

* allocation on a peer join or restart, under the waiver (`+UPW-iceoryx2-1+`), the permit whose waiver xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-waiver-approval[Waivers are approved with an interference argument] has the deployment's owner review and file;
* sizing of the expired-connection buffer: a receive route is refused at setup unless twice its retained budget fits in it;
* the service depth, which comes from deployment data (<<req-ssr-binding-depth-from-deployment,iceoryx2's service depth comes from deployment data>>): the service depths are part of the deployment data that xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-deployment-data[Deployment data is in range, verified and protected] covers;
* the port mutex's panic path, on lock and on unlock, which no peer can reach (`+UPD-IOX2-0001+`, revision 2);
* the two `+blocking_send+` `+unwrap+`s, which only a failing OS clock call reaches (`+UPD-IOX2-0002+`);
* iceoryx2's other accepted panic paths: two process-private invariants; a descriptor that other code in the process closes while a socket is set up; `+close()+` failing on a departed peer's descriptor; and a sample counter's overflow on 64-bit targets, at over 580 years at 10^9^ sends per second (`+UPD-IOX2-0003+`, `+UPD-IOX2-0004+`, `+UPD-IOX2-0005+`);
* heap exhaustion during a peer change, which aborts, within the deployment's heap sizing under `+UPW-iceoryx2-1+` (`+UPD-IOX2-0006+`): on iceoryx2 an allocation that fails during a peer change aborts the process, so that sizing is the one xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-waiver-approval[Waivers are approved with an interference argument], "Heap sizing", requires;
* arrival reports from its receive thread, within that thread's period plus its stated pass bound, on a drained notification of the route's data ID together with a sample in the queue or taken since the previous pass, or on the queue turning non-empty;
* a sender preempted between `+send+` and its notification delays the report, unless the queue was empty at the previous pass; a notification that fails at the sender while the queue holds samples leaves an arrival unreported, so a miss may be counted for a gap no longer than the deadline, and the sender counts the failure;
* while a new publisher's data connection is being set up, its notification can be reported while older samples are queued;
* iceoryx2 0.9.2 only logs a data connection that cannot be set up, under its default handler, and retries it only when the publisher list changes. So the binding installs a degradation handler on each port at setup: a failed connection is counted as `+receive_failed+`, or on the sending route, and the route then reports only a queue that turned non-empty, until the publisher list changes and an update runs clean; a queue that a healthy second publisher keeps non-empty meanwhile shows misses;
* the event IDs are read from the event service's static config, and a route whose lifecycle events share the data ID, or whose data ID is above the service's `+event_id_max_value+`, is refused at setup.

*Known failures.*
The README also says that a frame drop can wait for the port mutex. That is not an accepted limit, because xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-frame-drop[What a received frame's destructor may do] forbids it. It is the binding's known failure `+UPF-IOX2-0001+`, open until the binding returns each dropped frame to iceoryx2 through its deferred return as pending cleanup, charged and isolated as xref:../seooc-up-rust/release.adoc#req-ssr-release-lease-drop[A lease gives back its slot and nothing else] requires; a thread of its own is not by itself that isolation.

iceoryx2's other known failures are not accepted limits either, because xref:bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-no-panic[No panic on any peer input] forbids them: its index bug on a connection change (`+UPF-IOX2-0002+`), until iceoryx2 fixes the index; its event-deadline panics, on QM transports only, since ASIL deployments turn those deadlines off, until iceoryx2 fixes them (`+UPF-IOX2-0005+`); and the paths through which a peer that writes values its own iceoryx2 never writes can panic its survivors, until iceoryx2 validates what peers write (`+UPF-IOX2-0004+`).

The wait thread's descriptor-set panics (`+UPF-IOX2-0006+`) remain until its own poll loop replaces that set, or a reviewed equivalent with a source-backed argument is adopted in its place, and unsafe name and queue-size configuration (`+UPF-IOX2-0007+`) remains until setup refuses it.

The expired-buffer failure is fixed by the twice-budget setup refusal; it is recorded separately as fixed, not as an accepted anomaly.

*Strict mode.*
In strict mode, the Enforce state refuses any known failure, whatever is acknowledged (xref:deployment.adoc#aou-seooc-qualified-bindings-and-wires[Bindings and wires pass their contracts]); while iceoryx2's known failures stand, that refuses each affected route at ASIL, including publishers affected by shared-state faults.
--

*Violation:*

* A route whose lifecycle events share its data ID, or whose data ID is above the service's range, is *refused at setup*.
* A known anomaly accepted here is not a waiver.
* A heap too small for the bound of `+UPW-iceoryx2-1+` aborts the process during a peer change, which is *undetectable* until then (xref:../seooc-up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-waiver-approval[Waivers are approved with an interference argument]).
* In strict mode, any known failure is *refused at setup* in the Enforce state.

[horizontal]
ID:: `+aou~seooc-iceoryx2-limits~1+`
ASIL:: B
Status:: Draft

*Rationale.*
An accepted anomaly has a bounded justification, whereas an open known failure remains a failure.

A faulty peer's shared-memory writes that reach a middleware panic are known failures too, whatever anomaly is acknowledged.

These are one binding's limits, so they are stated with that binding, and each general duty stays in its own assumption.
A known failure is the binding's to close: listing it here accepts nothing, and moves no duty of the binding to the integrator.

=== Where iceoryx2 is only the test bed or the example

Verification lines and rationales of other items name iceoryx2 as the real transport a test runs on, or as the source of an example.

* Those mentions restrict nothing, and they are not listed here.
* A test that runs on real iceoryx2 is evidence on iceoryx2.
It is no evidence for another binding, which covers the duties of every binding in its own repository (xref:bindings.adoc[allocated to each binding]).
