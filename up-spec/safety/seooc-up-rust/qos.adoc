[[req-ssr-qos-declared]]
== QoS is declared per route at setup

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-qos-declared~3",oft-title="QoS is declared per route at setup",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore"]
--
*The declaration.*
The element *MUST* take every route's QoS (reliability, overflow, depth, history, deadline, and whether priority is required) from a declaration made during initialization in transport-agnostic terms, or give the route the one documented default.

Priority and TTL stay per message.

*What each setting belongs to.*

* Reliability, overflow and history belong to the route: on one transport, every declaration of one route agrees on them (<<req-ssr-qos-consistent-declarations,Two declarations of one route agree>>).
* Depth is the capacity of the declaring endpoint's queue: the number of frames that it holds before the overflow policy applies.
* A deadline is the declaring receive endpoint's monitoring of arrivals (<<req-ssr-qos-deadline,Deadlines are monitored>>).
* Whether priority is required is a demand on the capability of the route's binding.

*A deadline or `+event_only()+`.*
A declaration gives a deadline, declares `+event_only()+`, or does neither.
The clauses of this paragraph hold for every route but a detector route of the E2E wire.

* An explicit `+event_only()+` declaration permits a route without a deadline, in every state of strict mode.
* The element *MUST* refuse at setup, in every state of strict mode, a declaration that gives a deadline together with `+event_only()+`, naming the route and the setting.
* In strict mode's Off and Report states, the element *MUST NOT* refuse a route solely because it declares neither.
* In strict mode's Enforce and Locked states, the element *MUST* refuse at setup, as a forbidden finding (xref:build.adoc#req-ssr-strict-states[Strict mode has four states]), an ASIL receive route that declares neither (xref:../up-l1/integrity.adoc#req-ssr-integrity-declared[Each transport declares one integrity level]; xref:assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-timing-budgets[Timing claims rest on a supported configuration, and the item's budgets are the integrator's]).
In the Report state, the element *MUST* report such a route as that finding (xref:build.adoc#req-ssr-strict-report[Strict mode reports on every run]).

*A detector route of the E2E wire.*
A detector route of the E2E wire is a receive route that selects the E2E wire and on which that wire judges each declared sender's counters: a topic's or a notification's receive route, and an RPC server's request route (xref:e2e-wire.adoc#req-ssr-e2e-detects[The E2E wire detects each fault]).

* The element *MUST* refuse at setup, in every state of strict mode and at every integrity level, a detector route of the E2E wire that declares `+event_only()+` or no deadline, naming the route and the setting (xref:assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-e2e-protection[The integrator decides which routes need end-to-end protection]).
* The element *MUST* refuse such a route that gives a deadline together with `+event_only()+`, as it refuses any other.
* The route's deadline is the timeout of its end-to-end detection.
The element monitors it as <<req-ssr-qos-deadline,Deadlines are monitored>> states; that the E2E wire keeps no timeout of its own is stated in xref:e2e-wire.adoc#req-ssr-e2e-detects[The E2E wire detects each fault].
* An RPC client's response route is no detector route: the E2E wire keeps no receiving slot for it (xref:e2e-wire.adoc#req-ssr-e2e-receiver-state[E2E receiving state is one fixed slot per declared data ID]), and each call ends by its TTL (xref:../up-l2/interaction.adoc#req-ssr-peer-rpc-ends[RPC calls always end]).
The clauses on a deadline or `+event_only()+` apply to it.
* In strict mode's Enforce and Locked states, the element *MUST* refuse at setup, as a forbidden finding (xref:build.adoc#req-ssr-strict-states[Strict mode has four states]), at every integrity level, an RPC client's response route that selects the E2E wire and declares neither, naming the route and the setting.
In the Report state, the element *MUST* report such a route as that finding.
* This paragraph asks no deadline of a route that only sends.
--

*Rationale.*
A route that declares `+event_only()+` forgoes deadline-based silence and delay detection by choice (xref:assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-timing-budgets[Timing claims rest on a supported configuration, and the item's budgets are the integrator's]).
The declaration lets the Enforce and Locked states tell that choice from a missing deadline.

A declaration that gives both a deadline and `+event_only()+` makes no choice, so setup refuses it.

A counter reveals a lost frame only when a later frame arrives.
So the loss of a sender's last frames, and its silence, show only as a missed deadline, which is why a detector route cannot forgo one.
A client's response is judged against its call, and the call ends by its TTL, so a response route needs no deadline to show a silent server.
As on every receive route on the E2E wire, the Enforce and Locked states still ask it for an explicit choice.

Depth, a deadline and a required priority are each one endpoint's, so declarations of one route may differ in them.

[[req-ssr-qos-no-downgrade]]
== QoS is honored or refused, never weakened

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-qos-no-downgrade~5",oft-title="QoS is honored or refused, never weakened",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore"]
--
*Functional QoS.*
The element *MUST* check each route's QoS at setup against the declared capability of the route's binding, before any resource is reserved.
If it cannot be honored, the element *MUST* refuse setup, naming the route and the setting.
The element *MUST NOT* let a QoS setting be weakened silently, at setup or in runtime mode.

*Timing and assurance.*
A finite timing bound and a safety-assurance scope are claims separate from a route's functional QoS.
A finite timing bound is a stated endpoint reached within a bound, under a supported configuration whose execution model, workload envelope and evidence establish it.
A safety-assurance scope is the element's contribution to the safety requirements of an item, in ISO 26262's sense, at a stated ASIL and context, with its requirements, analyses, verification, qualification and assessment.

* Where a route explicitly requires either claim, the element *MUST* admit that claim only under the declared conditions of a supported configuration whose evidence establishes it.
Otherwise the element *MUST* refuse the route at setup, naming the route, the claim and the missing condition.
* The element *MUST NOT* let either claim be inferred from an OS family, an architecture or a compiler.
* An integrity level, an ASIL label or a strict-mode state is no request for either claim.
* Apart from the refusal that the last entry states for strict mode's Enforce and Locked states, the element *MUST NOT* refuse a route that requires only functional QoS for want of an optional timing bound or assurance scope.
* Where a configuration offers no finite bound for an endpoint, the element *MUST* still operate and report functionally on routes admitted under this item.
The first and last entries state which routes setup refuses.
* In strict mode's Enforce and Locked states, the element *MUST* refuse at setup, as a forbidden finding (xref:build.adoc#req-ssr-strict-states[Strict mode has four states]), a route that declares a deadline and a reaction budget where its detection bound exceeds that budget or no finite detection bound is established for it (<<req-ssr-qos-deadline,Deadlines are monitored>>; xref:assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-timing-budgets[Timing claims rest on a supported configuration, and the item's budgets are the integrator's]).
In the Report state, the element *MUST* report such a route as that finding (xref:build.adoc#req-ssr-strict-report[Strict mode reports on every run]) and *MUST NOT* refuse it for that finding.
A declared reaction budget is by itself no requirement of a finite timing bound in the sense of the first entry.

*Reporting.*
The element *MUST* state separately in each route's report:

* its admitted functional QoS;
* any admitted finite timing bound, with the configuration it rests on;
* any admitted assurance scope.

In a report, the element *MUST NOT* present a passed setup check as a safety qualification.
--

*Rationale.*
Silently substituting a supported policy would leave the application relying on a guarantee the route does not provide.

Functional communication, a finite timing bound and a safety-assurance scope are independent properties (xref:../README.adoc#req-ssr-contract-transition[What a part declares and claims, and when a replacement takes effect], clause "What a claim covers").
Ordinary use needs only the first.
Refusing it for want of the others would make a safety package a prerequisite for non-safety use.
Granting a required stronger claim without its evidence would be a silent downgrade of that claim.

[[req-ssr-qos-overflow]]
== Overflow acts as declared

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-qos-overflow~3",oft-title="Overflow acts as declared",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore"]
--
*The policy.*
In runtime mode the element *MUST* ensure that a full route applies exactly its declared overflow policy (drop the oldest, drop the newest, or wait within a bound).
The element *MUST* count every discard observable to it or to the route's binding, exactly once under its cause.

*No heap for the policy itself.*
The element *MUST* ensure that choosing and applying the policy, waiting and counting acquire and release no heap memory.

*Native and heap-free routes.*
On a native route, and on any route whose heap class is none, the element *MUST* ensure that a discard releases nothing.

*Routes that declare allocation.*
On a serialized route whose declared heap class admits allocation, the policy ends ownership of one of these:

* an evicted oldest value;
* a refused newest frame;
* a waiting sender's frame whose bound ran out.

That ownership is released exactly once as xref:heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-slot-types[A decoded value is destroyed once, as its route's class admits] and xref:../up-l1/bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-frame-drop[What a received frame's destructor may do] state.
The element *MUST* ensure that this release is the only heap use an overflow causes, and that it releases no route, table or other runtime resource.

*Discards the binding cannot observe.*
If a discard occurs entirely inside middleware and the binding cannot observe it, the element *MUST* explicitly disclose that limitation in the route's report and make no claim there to count it (xref:errors.adoc#req-ssr-error-no-silent-loss[No silent loss, and each loss counted once]).
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-qos-overflow~3+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the element

* *test*
** The QoS contract suite, observing what an application receives from the documented read call alone, with no drain loop first.
** Observable discards counted once under their cause.

*Rationale.*
Counting observable discards gives the application a local congestion signal.

Counter fields can be present for events that a binding cannot observe.

_Heap use on overflow._
Only the release of the ownership that the policy ends, under the route's declared class, may touch the heap.
Destroying an evicted value on a serialized route that admits owning values can touch the heap.

[[req-ssr-qos-deadline]]
== Deadlines are monitored

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-qos-deadline~6",oft-title="Deadlines are monitored",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore,SafetyBinding"]
--
*Terms.*

* An *arrival* is a frame that reaches the route's queue.
A frame that the overflow policy then discards has arrived, and so has one that is refused when it is read.
A frame that is refused before it enters the queue has not arrived; on a push binding that includes a frame whose time-to-live has already run out.
A read, a carrier's take and a wake hint are no arrivals.
* The *arrival latency* L is the longest time that the route's binding declares from an arrival to its report of that arrival to the element (xref:../up-l1/bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-arrival-reports[A binding reports each arrival within the latency that it declares, and each event as what it is]).
On a push binding, where the element records an arrival at delivery, L is zero.
* The *threshold* is the route's deadline D plus L.
* The monitor's *resolution* r is the step in which it measures a gap.
The element *MUST NOT* let r exceed 1/512 of the threshold.
* A *gap* runs from one recorded arrival to the next.
A route's first gap runs from its transport's entry into runtime mode, which is no arrival, to its first recorded arrival.

Bad data is reported by its own refusal counters.

*Recording arrivals.*

* On a push binding the element *MUST* record an arrival at delivery.
* On a pull binding the element *MUST* record an arrival when the binding reports it.
* The element *MUST NOT* record an arrival for a read, for a carrier's take or for a wake hint.

What a binding owes for the latency that it declares and for its arrival reports is stated in xref:../up-l1/bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-arrival-reports[A binding reports each arrival within the latency that it declares, and each event as what it is].

*What is counted.*
On a route that declares a deadline, the element *MUST* count misses by the length of each gap, measured from its start to the recorded arrival that ends it, or to a check while the gap is open:

* it *MUST NOT* count a miss for a gap no longer than the threshold;
* it *MUST* count one miss for a gap longer than the threshold by more than r;
* for a gap longer than the threshold by no more than r, it *MAY* count one miss or none;
* it *MUST NOT* count any gap more than once: a gap is counted by a check during the gap, or by the arrival that ends it.

So the element counts no miss for a true gap no longer than the deadline while every arrival is recorded within L of the time at which it arrived.
That condition fails where an arrival is reported later than L, is not reported, or is recorded late; neither L nor the bound J makes allowance for such an arrival.

*Saturation.*
The element *MUST NOT* let the count wrap: at the end of its range it *MUST* saturate the count, and *MUST* say so in the route's report.

*Suspended threads.*
With a monitor that keeps full-width timestamps and counts, the element *MUST* keep these properties however long a thread that records, checks or reports for the route is suspended.
In a resumed operation, the element *MUST NOT* count a gap twice, credit a later gap with a stale observation or publish a torn count.

*Finite-range representations.*
For a monitor representation that is wrap-aware over a finite range of ticks, these properties hold only within the servicing and waiting range that its supported configuration states.
For the one-word tick representation that range is:

* the route serviced at least every 2^29^ ticks of its monitor (xref:assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-application-reacts[The application reacts to what the element reports]);
* every wait inside the element under 2^28^ ticks (xref:assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-timing-budgets[Timing claims rest on a supported configuration, and the item's budgets are the integrator's]).

The element *MUST* ensure that the transport's `+wait+` services its routes between inner waits of at most 2^28^ ticks of the shortest-scaled route on the transport.

A transport's servicing bound is the shortest servicing range of its monitored routes.
A transport whose monitors all keep full width has none.

*A declared servicing period.*
In strict mode's Enforce and Locked states, the element *MUST* refuse at setup, as a forbidden finding (xref:build.adoc#req-ssr-strict-states[Strict mode has four states]), a declared servicing period longer than its transport's servicing bound.
In the Report state, the element *MUST* report such a period as that finding (xref:build.adoc#req-ssr-strict-report[Strict mode reports on every run]) and *MUST NOT* refuse setup for that finding.

*When the count is current.*
The element *MUST* ensure that the count is current whenever the route's counters are read after the transport's receive, wait or service operations.
So the count does not depend on the application polling that route.

*The report.*
The element *MUST* state in a monitored route's report the route's deadline D, the declared arrival latency L of the route's binding and the monitor's resolution r, each with its unit.

* J is a bound, in a supported configuration, on every path that can win the count's update and publish it: the service, check and arrival paths, their contention and the publication.
It covers no arrival report, no recording of an arrival and no read by an application.
* Where a supported configuration establishes a finite J, the element *MUST* state in the report the detection bound D + 2L + r + J.
That bound runs from the last true arrival, or, before any arrival, from the transport's entry into runtime mode, to the winning counter update and its visibility under the published read-ordering contract.
* Where no configuration establishes J, the element *MUST* state in the report that no finite detection bound is established, and monitoring and counting still operate.

An application's later read of the report adds its own delay, outside J.
Reading the report is no servicing: this item sets no rate at which an application reads it, and the servicing that a finite-range representation needs is stated under "Finite-range representations".

*Middleware event deadlines.*
In a configuration on the ASIL path (xref:README.adoc#req-ssr-element-configurations[The ASIL path and supported configurations]), each binding *MUST* turn its middleware's own event deadlines off.
The element's deadline monitor is the receiver's mechanism.

*Related rules.*

* The deadlines that setup refuses are stated in <<req-ssr-qos-invalid-refused,Meaningless QoS is refused at setup>>.
* What setup does with a route that declares no deadline, or a deadline together with `+event_only()+`, and which routes of the E2E wire need one, is stated in <<req-ssr-qos-declared,QoS is declared per route at setup>>.
--

*Rationale.*
Arrival-based monitoring observes communication independently of application reads.
An overflowed or undecodable frame still arrived, with observable discards or refusals reported separately.

On a shared route another peer can keep this aggregate deadline refreshed while one peer is starved (xref:assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-peer-rates[Peer rates on shared routes are the integrator's]).
So the absence of a miss there shows no single peer's progress.

A sender that never sends leaves no arrival to measure from, so a route's first gap starts when its transport enters runtime mode.

_The band._
A monitor measures a gap in steps of its resolution, so it cannot tell a gap just over the threshold from one at it.
The band of width r above the threshold lets it count such a gap or not, keeps the edge at the threshold exact and bounds by r what can go uncounted.

_Full width._
A monitor that keeps full-width origins and a saturating full-width count has no wrap window.
That is why the statement requires its properties to hold however long a thread that records, checks or reports for the route is suspended, with no watchdog and no servicing range.
Full width removes the wrap window only.

Where a representation needs a servicing range, the range is stated with its configuration and applies to that configuration only.

_The bound J._
J depends on how a configuration schedules and interrupts the service, check and arrival paths, and on their contention and publication, which the monitor cannot establish alone.
So the element states the detection bound only where a supported configuration establishes J, and otherwise says that it is not established.
The arithmetic that shows a monitor correct under suspension bounds no path's time, so it establishes no J.

_Twice L._
One L allows for the last true arrival being recorded up to L late.
The other is the threshold's allowance for the record of the next arrival.

[[req-ssr-qos-ttl]]
== Expired messages are not processed

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-qos-ttl~6",oft-title="Expired messages are not processed",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore,SafetyBinding"]
--
*TTL.*
A message's TTL is its `+ttl+` attribute, a whole number of milliseconds, counted from the time that its message ID records (xref:../../basics/uattributes.adoc#common-attributes[UAttributes, "Common Message Attributes"]).
A TTL of 0 means that the message does not expire (`+dsn~up-attributes-ttl~1+`).
Where the element takes a TTL in a unit finer than a millisecond, it *MUST* set the `+ttl+` attribute of a message whose TTL is positive to that TTL rounded up to a whole millisecond, and never to 0.

*Expired frames.*
A frame whose TTL is greater than 0 expires as this item states.
The element *MUST NOT* deliver an expired frame to the application, or let a carrier (xref:../up-l2/streamer.adoc#req-ssr-carrier-route[A carrier route takes each frame at its ingress and sends it at its egress]) forward it, and *MUST* count it as expired.

*A TTL of 0.*
The element *MUST NOT* refuse a frame whose TTL is 0 as expired, or count it as expired, however much time has passed: when the frame reaches its route, when it is taken, and before a carrier forwards it.
The clauses "How expiry is judged" and "An unreadable clock" apply to a TTL greater than 0 only.
A frame whose TTL is 0 needs no reading of the clock, and the element *MUST NOT* refuse it because the clock cannot be read.

*RPC requests.*
The protocol requires an RPC request's TTL to be greater than 0 (`+dsn~up-attributes-request-ttl~1+`), and a call ends by that TTL (xref:../up-l2/interaction.adoc#req-ssr-peer-rpc-ends[RPC calls always end]), so a request whose TTL is 0 is invalid, not expired.
The element *MUST* refuse an RPC request whose TTL is 0:

* at a client, before the request is sent, with an error that names the TTL;
* at a server, when the request reaches its route and before its wire decodes it: the element *MUST NOT* deliver it to the application, and *MUST* count it once, under a cause that names the TTL and not as expired.

*How expiry is judged.*
For a frame whose TTL is greater than 0, the element *MUST* judge expiry in whole milliseconds of wall time, from the time the frame's message ID records.
Measured from that time:

* the element *MUST NOT* refuse the frame before its TTL has passed;
* the element *MUST* always refuse the frame once its TTL, rounded up to a whole millisecond, plus one more millisecond has passed.

An ID made while the sender's wall clock could not be read records the last good time, so its frame can be refused before its TTL has passed in real time, by up to the time the clock was unreadable.
For a generator that keeps the last good time only to 64 ms, such a frame can be refused by up to 64 ms more (xref:../up-l2/interaction.adoc#req-ssr-ids-unique[Message IDs are unique]).

*An unreadable clock.*
If the wall clock cannot be read at the check of a frame whose TTL is greater than 0, the element *MUST* refuse the frame and count it once, under a cause that names the unreadable clock and not as expired.

*When expiry is checked.*
The element *MUST* check expiry when a frame reaches its route, before its wire decodes it, as well as when it is taken.
So an expired frame is never decoded and changes no wire state.

When a frame reaches its route, and again when it is taken, the element *MUST* make the checks of this item that apply then, of its expiry, of an RPC request's TTL of 0 and of an unreadable clock, before any check of the encoding or the presence of the frame's payload that it makes at that point (xref:../up-l1/payload-paths.adoc#req-ssr-wire-encoding-checked[A wire is given only a payload of its own encoding]).
For a frame that one of them refuses, the element *MUST NOT* then check the encoding or the presence of its payload, give it a `+missing_payload+` result or count it under another cause: its one count is that check's, and that item's refusal, result and count for its payload do not apply to it.

*External lifetimes.*
A binding whose middleware carries a lifetime of its own for a message *MUST* state in its declaration how it maps a TTL to that lifetime and back, a zero and an absent value included, *MUST NOT* turn an external value that means immediate expiry into a TTL of 0, and *MUST NOT* turn a TTL of 0 into an external value that means immediate expiry.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-qos-ttl~6+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the element; each binding, for its mapping of a TTL to and from a lifetime of its middleware's own
Verification:: test, review
* *test*
** A frame whose TTL is greater than 0, at receive and before forwarding.
** A frame whose TTL is greater than 0, at the bound, for a whole-millisecond TTL and for a TTL taken finer than a millisecond, which is set rounded up and never as 0.
** A frame whose TTL is greater than 0, with the clock unavailable: refused, and counted once under its own cause and not as expired.
** A frame whose TTL is greater than 0 and has expired, refused when it reaches its route, never decoded, with no end-to-end state changed.
** A frame whose TTL is greater than 0 and whose ID was made while the sender's wall clock could not be read, at the bound of its early refusal, also from a generator that keeps the last good time only to 64 ms.
** A frame whose TTL is 0, held far beyond every bound above, with an old time in its message ID and with the wall clock unreadable: not refused and not counted as expired, when it reaches its route, when it is taken and before a carrier forwards it.
** An RPC request whose TTL is 0: refused at the client before it is sent, with an error that names the TTL; at a server, refused when it reaches its route, never decoded and with no end-to-end state changed, not delivered, and counted once under its own cause and not as expired.
** An expired frame and a frame whose TTL is greater than 0 checked while the clock cannot be read, each when it reaches its route, and an RPC request whose TTL is 0 when it reaches a server's route, each with a payload of an encoding that no wire of the route declares and each with no payload: refused before the encoding or the presence of its payload is checked, and counted once under that check's cause, with no mismatch, `+missing_payload+` or `+unprotected+` result or count, on a route that does not select the E2E wire and on one that does.
** The same for an expired frame and a frame whose TTL is greater than 0 checked while the clock cannot be read, each when it is taken, with a payload of an encoding that no wire of the route declares where the element checks the encoding of a payload only when a frame is taken, and with no payload where it checks the presence of a payload only then.
** For a binding whose middleware has a lifetime of its own: the mapping that its declaration states, with an absent value, a zero and a positive value in both directions; an external value that means immediate expiry never becoming a TTL of 0; and a TTL of 0 never becoming an external value that means immediate expiry.
* *review*: the expiry-check paths never refuse or count a frame whose TTL is 0 as expired, and never refuse it because the wall clock cannot be read.

*Rationale.*
The check also reads the current time in whole milliseconds, so it can see up to one millisecond less than has passed.
The bound allows for that millisecond.

A frame that is refused because the clock cannot be read has not been shown to be expired, so it is counted under a cause of its own.

A sender that cannot read its clock reuses the last good time in its IDs, so they keep working (xref:../up-l2/interaction.adoc#req-ssr-ids-unique[Message IDs are unique]).

Expiry is judged at the element's checks, when a frame reaches its route and when it is taken.
TTL decides which frames those checks accept.
It does not bound when a descheduled reader next takes a frame, or when an application reacts to one it has taken, which is a timing claim of a supported configuration (xref:bounded-work.adoc#req-ssr-bound-wait[Every bounded wait has a logical deadline]).

A check of a TTL reads only the frame's attributes, so it comes before any look at the payload, and the first check that refuses a frame names the cause of its one count.

A TTL of 0 is the protocol's value for a message that does not expire, and an RPC call needs a deadline, so a request cannot use it.
Refusing such a request as invalid calls no message with a TTL of 0 expired.

[[req-ssr-qos-invalid-refused]]
== Meaningless QoS is refused at setup

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-qos-invalid-refused~2",oft-title="Meaningless QoS is refused at setup",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore"]
--
Six QoS declarations cannot be honored as written, and the element *MUST* refuse each at setup, naming the route and the setting:

* a zero deadline;
* a deadline under 1,024 ns;
* a deadline on a pull route whose binding cannot report arrivals (xref:../up-l1/bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-arrival-reports[A binding reports each arrival within the latency that it declares, and each event as what it is]);
* a deadline shorter than the route's binding's declared arrival latency;
* a wait policy with a zero timeout;
* history on an RPC route.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-qos-invalid-refused~2+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the element
Verification:: test (each of the six refusals)

*Rationale.*
Diagnosing a declaration outside the monitor or binding capability before activation keeps it from becoming a different policy at runtime.

A deadline on a pull route whose binding cannot report arrivals is refused whatever timing claim the route makes, because the monitor counts from the arrivals that the binding reports.

[[req-ssr-qos-consistent-declarations]]
== Two declarations of one route agree

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-qos-consistent-declarations~2",oft-title="Two declarations of one route agree",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore"]
--
On one transport, the element *MUST* refuse at setup two declarations of the same route that differ in reliability, overflow or history, naming the route and the setting.

The element *MUST NOT* refuse two declarations of the same route because they differ in depth, in deadline or in whether priority is required.
Each of those settings is one endpoint's, and the element checks each declaration's own against the capability of the route's binding (<<req-ssr-qos-declared,QoS is declared per route at setup>>; <<req-ssr-qos-no-downgrade,QoS is honored or refused, never weakened>>).
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-qos-consistent-declarations~2+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the element
Verification:: test
* On one transport, two declarations of one route that differ in reliability, in overflow and in history, each refused, naming the route and the setting.
* Two declarations of one route that agree on those three settings and differ in depth, in deadline and in whether priority is required, alone and together: admitted, each endpoint keeping its own.
* One of them with a setting beyond the capability of the route's binding: refused for that setting, and not as a conflict.

*Rationale.*
Two declarations of one route that differ in reliability, overflow or history cannot both be honored, and choosing one would silently weaken the other (<<req-ssr-qos-no-downgrade,QoS is honored or refused, never weakened>>).
So setup refuses, and the refusal names the setting in conflict.

Depth, a deadline and a required priority are each one endpoint's, so two declarations can both be honored where they differ in them.
