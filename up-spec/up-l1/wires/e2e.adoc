[[req-ssr-e2e-not-required]]
== The element requires end-to-end protection on no route, outside strict mode's black-channel rule

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-not-required~2",oft-title="The element requires end-to-end protection on no route, outside strict mode's black-channel rule",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore"]
--
*No universal duty.*
The element *MUST NOT* refuse a route because it has no end-to-end protection, at any integrity level and in any state of strict mode, except as the next paragraph states.
The element *MUST NOT* require that a route's protection be the E2E wire.

*A black channel.*
In strict mode's Enforce and Locked states, an ASIL route over a transport that the integrator classifies as a black channel is refused where it names no protection (xref:../../safety/elements/up-rust/strict-mode.adoc#req-ssr-strict-rules[The rules that strict mode checks]).
Naming the integrator's own protection meets that rule, and so does selecting the E2E wire for the route.

*A route that selects the E2E wire.*
A route selects the E2E wire by its declaration at setup.
What an item requires of the E2E wire on a route applies only where that route selects the wire.
What an item requires of the element or the Streamer for a frame that the E2E wire protects applies to each frame that the wire encoded on such a route, on every hop that carries it.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-not-required~2+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the element
Verification:: test (in strict mode's Off and Report states, a route at an ASIL integrity level with no end-to-end protection, admitted at setup; in the Enforce and Locked states, an ASIL route with no end-to-end protection over a transport that the integrator does not classify as a black channel, an ASIL route over a black-channel transport whose declaration names the integrator's own protection, and an ASIL route over a black-channel transport that selects the E2E wire, each admitted)

*Rationale.*
Which routes need end-to-end protection is the integrator's decision for the item, in ISO 26262's sense (xref:../../safety/elements/up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-e2e-protection[The integrator decides which routes need end-to-end protection]).
Requiring it in the element would make protection a condition even of routes that need none.

[[req-ssr-e2e-wraps-any-wire]]
== The E2E wire wraps any wire and leaves its bytes alone

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-wraps-any-wire~5",oft-title="The E2E wire wraps any wire and leaves its bytes alone",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
The E2E wire, `+E2e<W>+`, *MUST* wrap any ordinary wire `+W+` that meets its wire contract on its own, for the same message type.

*The header.*
The E2E wire *MUST* add a header, 32 bytes on a topic and 48 on an RPC route, in the formats of <<req-ssr-e2e-header-format,The E2E header has one documented format>>.
In this E2E protection contract, a topic is a route of published messages or of notifications.
The header *MUST* carry a data ID, a counter, its sender's incarnation, the frame's length, and a CRC over every other header field and the inner payload.
A method header also carries the fields that <<req-ssr-e2e-header-format,The E2E header has one documented format>> adds.

*The inner wire.*
The E2E wire *MUST* leave the inner wire's encoded bytes unchanged.

*What it states about itself.*

* The E2E wire *MUST* state its own wire identity, and its own payload encoding from the private-use range.
* Its maximum encoded length *MUST* include the header, and it *MUST* be exact when the inner wire is.
* The E2E wire *MUST NOT* add heap use of its own, and *MUST* state the inner wire's heap claim for each direction.

*Two wires, two contracts.*

* The E2E wire is a sequenced wire (xref:README.adoc#req-ssr-wire-scope[What a wire is, and the wire contract]), and *MUST* meet the wire contract as a sequenced wire meets it.
* The selected inner wire is an ordinary wire, and the two wires are a composition, in which each keeps its own wire contract (xref:README.adoc#req-ssr-wire-scope[What a wire is, and the wire contract]).
A requirement on a wire's heap class applies to it under that requirement's own condition (xref:README.adoc#req-ssr-wire-heap-claim-tested[A heap-free claim is true, and encoding is repeatable]).
* A duty that an item gives the E2E wire is no duty of the inner wire.

*The rules for serialized wires.*
The inner wire, where it is a serialized wire, stays bound by xref:serialized.adoc#req-ssr-wire-serialized-format[A serialized wire reads and writes one stated format] and xref:serialized.adoc#req-ssr-wire-encode-decode[A serialized wire encodes once into the storage it is given, and decodes into a value that its slot owns] for its own encoding.
Neither item binds the E2E wire: for the E2E wire, this item replaces revision 1 of each of them, which named each serialized wire.
For its own format, sizing, encoding and decoding, the E2E wire *MUST* meet each duty that those two items give a serialized wire, with these two replacements:

* *What it accepts.* A serialized wire accepts each input that its format makes an encoding, and its result depends on the input alone.
Whether the E2E wire accepts or refuses such a frame is decided instead by its protection checks and its receiving state, as <<req-ssr-e2e-detects,The E2E wire detects each fault>> and the items that it links state.
The value that an accepted frame decodes to still depends on the input alone, and not on what the slot held before.
* *The order of its writes.* A serialized wire writes its encoding once, in order.
The E2E wire *MAY* instead write its header after the inner payload, which its CRC covers.
The inner payload is still written once, in order, directly into the storage that carries the frame: this permission allows no second encoding of it and no copy of it.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-wraps-any-wire~5+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the E2E wire
Verification:: test, heap-window
* *test*
** The sequenced wire contract suite on the E2E wire, over a serialized wire and over a test wire.
** A control: the wire contract suite's check that every encode of an ordinary wire is repeatable refuses the E2E wire, with that check's diagnostic.
** A byte comparison showing the inner encoding unchanged inside the frame.
** The wire's stated identity, payload encoding, maximum encoded length and its exactness, and the inner wire's heap claim, stated for each direction.
** Each inner wire of these cases passing the ordinary wire contract suite on its own.
** The duties of the two serialized-wire items that the E2E wire keeps, on the E2E wire itself: its largest encoding and an exact size against the bytes written; where it states a smallest encoding, no encoding that it writes shorter; a value too large for the route's bound refused, with nothing sent and the value unchanged; a value left unchanged by each encode; a value that the inner wire cannot represent, an error with nothing sent; an accepted frame decoded into a slot that held each of several earlier values, with the same value each time and each earlier value ended exactly once; a refused frame, and a frame whose inner decode fails, each leaving a valid value in the slot and nothing half-owned; a decoded value used after its frame was returned.
** The header written after the inner payload: that payload written once, at the place in the frame's storage where it is carried, with no second encoding and no copy of it.

*Rationale.*
Wrapping preserves the inner wire as a separate choice, and the wrapper can claim only the heap behavior and size guarantees that the combined encoding supports.

[[req-ssr-e2e-header-format]]
== The E2E header has one documented format

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-header-format~3",oft-title="The E2E header has one documented format",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
The E2E wire's headers *MUST* have two fixed formats, each with a 64-bit CRC, documented with the crate:

* a 32-byte data header for topics;
* a 48-byte method header for RPC routes, which adds a Source ID, a message type, a result and a status.

The record *MUST* cover:

* the field order, the field sizes and the byte order;
* the values of the method header's fields, a refusal answer's results and kinds among them;
* the CRC's polynomial, initial value, reflection and final XOR;
* known-answer vectors for each header and the CRC;
* the CRC's Hamming distance at the largest frame the wire admits, from which an integrator works out each route's residual error rate.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-header-format~3+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the E2E wire
Verification:: test (the known-answer vectors, encoded and decoded), review (the stated Hamming distance, against the CRC's published properties)

*Rationale.*
A fixed, documented header permits independent implementations to check the same protected bytes.
Undetected header corruption remains part of the residual error analysis.


[[req-ssr-e2e-detects]]
== The E2E wire detects each fault

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-detects~7",oft-title="The E2E wire detects each fault",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
*Header, CRC and data ID first.*
Decoding through the E2E wire *MUST* first check each frame's header, its CRC and its data ID.

* The E2E wire *MUST* refuse as corrupt a frame whose length field does not match its size, or whose CRC does not match.
* A frame whose data ID was not declared is refused as <<req-ssr-e2e-data-id,One data ID per sender and route>> states.
* Either refusal *MUST NOT* change any state.

*Method fields.*
The E2E wire *MUST* check a request's method fields before its counter, *MUST* refuse as corrupt a request that fails them, and *MUST NOT* change any state for it.
What a request carries in them is stated in <<req-ssr-e2e-rpc-echo,An E2E response answers exactly its call>>.

*Corrupt comes first.*
The E2E wire *MUST* refuse as corrupt each frame that it finds corrupt, whatever data ID the frame names.
It refuses a frame as an undeclared data ID only where it does not find the frame corrupt.

*The counter.*
On every route but a response route, the E2E wire, as the receiver, then judges the counter, for each declared sender:

* the first frame after the receiver starts *MUST* be delivered, and its incarnation and counter *MUST* start the reference;
* a frame of the reference's incarnation is judged through the window, as <<req-ssr-e2e-replay-window,A sliding window judges a reference's counters>> states;
* a frame of another incarnation is judged as <<req-ssr-e2e-resync,A restart resynchronizes, stale frames are refused, and no arrival order locks a sender out>> states.

A response is judged by <<req-ssr-e2e-rpc-echo,An E2E response answers exactly its call>>.

*After the checks.*

* A frame that passes these checks and then fails the inner wire's decode *MUST* have advanced the reference.
* Each refusal *MUST* name what failed, without heap and without panicking.
* Which refused requests are answered, and how, is stated in <<req-ssr-e2e-refusal-answered,A refused request is answered, final or advisory>> and xref:../../up-l2/api.adoc#req-ssr-peer-rpc-undecodable-answered[An undecodable request is answered].

*No timeout of its own.*
The E2E wire *MUST NOT* keep a timeout of its own: it judges only the frames that it is given.
How the element requires a detector route's deadline at setup, and monitors it as the timeout of end-to-end detection, is stated in xref:../runtime.adoc#req-ssr-qos-declared[QoS is declared per route at setup] and xref:../bindings.adoc#req-ssr-qos-deadline[Deadlines are monitored].
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-detects~7+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the E2E wire
Verification:: test, fault-injection, heap-window, review
* *test*
** After a receiver restart, each declared sender's first frame is delivered.
** Corruption is refused as corrupt.
** A corrupt frame that names an undeclared data ID, and a request whose method fields fail and that names one: each refused as corrupt, and not as an undeclared data ID.
** A request whose method fields fail is refused as corrupt, before its counter is judged.
** Each header check refuses, and changes no state.
** A frame that passes and then fails the inner decode advances the reference, and a later copy of it is refused as a repeat.
** With the receiver's last counter at exactly `+resync_frames+`, a restart is not refused as a repeat.
* *fault-injection*: a byte corrupted between hops; a torn copy on copy route.
* *review*: the E2E wire keeps no timeout of its own.
ISO 26262:: 6-Annex D.2.4 (part-clause numbers only)

*Rationale.*
Checking framing and integrity before changing receiver state keeps a corrupt header from becoming a valid sequence observation.
An undetected corruption remains a residual.

A wire that is given no route context cannot time a silence, so the route's deadline monitor does.

[[req-ssr-e2e-resync]]
== A restart resynchronizes, stale frames are refused, and no arrival order locks a sender out

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-resync~6",oft-title="A restart resynchronizes, stale frames are refused, and no arrival order locks a sender out",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
*Incarnations and declarations.*
Each frame carries its sender's incarnation (<<req-ssr-e2e-wraps-any-wire,The E2E wire wraps any wire and leaves its bytes alone>>).

* For each declared sender, the deployment data on both sides says whether its starts increase.
The E2E wire *MUST* treat a declared sender whose deployment data does not declare its starts increasing as not declared increasing.
* For each declared sender, the E2E wire *MUST* keep, as the receiver, the reference's incarnation and one run.
* For a sender declared increasing, it *MUST* also keep the newest incarnation adopted and one retired incarnation.

*The strict older-incarnation refusal.*
This refusal applies on an ASIL RPC request route that selects the E2E wire and is initialized with strict mode in its Enforce or Locked state, and on no other route.
Where it applies, the E2E wire, as the receiver, *MUST* refuse every request whose incarnation is older than the newest that it has adopted for that sender: as retired where that incarnation is the retired one, and as older otherwise.

* Such a request *MUST NOT* start or extend a resynchronization run, become the reference, or move the newest adopted incarnation backwards.
* A request that fails the framing or integrity checks is refused as corrupt first, as <<req-ssr-e2e-detects,The E2E wire detects each fault>> states.
* How such a refusal is counted and answered is stated in <<req-ssr-e2e-refusal-counted,End-to-end refusals are named, and counted once>> and <<req-ssr-e2e-refusal-answered,A refused request is answered, final or advisory>>.
* The refusal is runtime behavior that a setting fixed during initialization selects (xref:../../safety/elements/up-rust/strict-mode.adoc#req-ssr-strict-init-only[Strict mode checks in initialization only]), and the clients' increasing starts are the integrator's declaration (xref:../../safety/elements/up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-e2e-protection[The integrator decides which routes need end-to-end protection]).

*The run rule.*
Where the strict older-incarnation refusal applies, the run rule applies subject to it; everywhere else the run rule applies without it.

* *A sender declared increasing.* A frame of an incarnation newer than the newest adopted *MUST* be delivered at once: the reference's incarnation is retired, the reference moves to the frame, the run is cleared, and one restart is counted. A frame of the retired incarnation *MUST* be refused as stale. It *MUST NOT* start a run or be adopted.
* *Any other incarnation than the reference's.* Its frame *MUST* be refused as stale, unless it completes a run.
** The receiver keeps one run at a time.
** A frame of another incarnation than the run's *MUST* start a new run, at 0.
** A frame of the run's incarnation with a greater counter than the run's last frame *MUST* extend the run by one.
** A delivered frame of the reference's incarnation *MUST* end the run. So *MUST* the receiver's first frame, a restart, and an adoption.
** When the run reaches `+resync_frames+`, that frame *MUST* be adopted. It *MUST* be delivered, the reference *MUST* move to it, and one resync *MUST* be counted. For a sender declared increasing, the newest adopted and the retired incarnation *MUST* stay as they were.
** The frames refused before it *MUST NOT* be counted as lost.

*Restarts and arrival orders.*
While a sender's declaration is true:

* the restart of a sender declared increasing *MUST NOT* cost a refused frame;
* any other sender's restart *MUST* cost exactly `+resync_frames+` refused frames on a route that keeps order. They are counted from the last frame of the old incarnation to arrive, provided the new incarnation sends more than `+resync_frames+` frames;
* a live declared sender *MUST NOT* be refused for good because of the order in which its frames arrive.
Displaced incarnations are exceptions: the retired incarnation of a sender declared increasing is always refused, and where the strict older-incarnation refusal applies every incarnation older than the newest adopted is always refused.
An undetected corruption of a frame's header is no arrival order, and this duty does not cover what follows one (xref:../../safety/elements/up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-e2e-protection[The integrator decides which routes need end-to-end protection]).

*`+resync_frames+`.*
`+resync_frames+` is an unsigned 8-bit value from 1 to 255, set in deployment data.
At construction, the E2E wire *MUST* refuse a value outside that range, and *MUST* use 2 where deployment data sets none.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-resync~6+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the E2E wire
Verification:: fault-injection, test, heap-window
* *fault-injection*: a sender killed and restarted, with real processes.
** Declared increasing, with a counter supplied at construction: no frame refused and one restart counted.
** Not declared increasing: exactly `+resync_frames+` frames refused and one resync counted.
* *test*
** A sender's clock reset at boot, so that its new start reads earlier than its old one: with the old incarnation's late frames arriving among the new one's frames, and with the receiver restarted while those late frames are in flight, so that they arrive after the new incarnation's first frame; for each kind of sender, with the outcome that the run rule gives.
** Three starts of one sender within one millisecond, ordered by their entropy and not by their start values, with the frames of one that died delayed; for each kind of sender, with the outcome that the run rule gives.
** Every order in which the frames in flight of two dead incarnations and of the live one can arrive, each incarnation's frames in their own order, from each state of the receiver's slot and under each order of the three starts, without and with a frame of each incarnation duplicated.

Where the strict older-incarnation refusal does not apply, for a sender declared increasing whose declaration is false: the live incarnation refused as stale only once a frame of a newer incarnation has retired it while it was the reference, and from then on each of its frames refused as stale and counted; otherwise the first and the last assertion above hold.
Where that refusal applies, every live request older than the newest adopted is refused as retired or older under the strict older-incarnation rule, whether or not its incarnation was previously adopted.
** On a route that keeps order, while the sender's declaration is true, the restart of a sender declared increasing with no frame refused, and the restart of any other sender with exactly `+resync_frames+` frames refused, counted from the last frame of the old incarnation to arrive, where the new incarnation sends more than `+resync_frames+` frames.
** The sequenced wire contract's check that no arrival order refuses a live declared sender for good while its declaration is true.
** A control on the restart check of the sequenced wire contract suite: a wire whose receiver never resynchronizes after a sender's restart fails it, at the restart bound that the E2E wire gives the check, `+resync_frames+` refused frames.

** With increasing starts A < B < C, adopt A, then B, then C and deliver delayed valid A requests with counters 0, 1 and 2, all within TTL, at `+resync_frames=2+`.
Where the strict older-incarnation refusal applies: each A request refused as older and counted, and answered advisory because A is older than retired B and non-delivery cannot be proved; A never adopted; a fresh C request still delivered.
Where it does not apply: the first two A frames refused, and A adopted on the third.
** Where that refusal applies, requests of retired B, refused as retired, and of never-adopted incarnations between B and C, refused as older, get final answers only in the cases that <<req-ssr-e2e-refusal-answered,A refused request is answered, final or advisory>> lists.
** A declared sender whose deployment data does not declare its starts increasing, treated as not declared increasing.
** `+resync_frames+` not set, taken as 2.
** The strict older-incarnation refusal not applied in the Off and Report states, on a QM route, or on a topic.
** `+resync_frames+` of 0, refused at construction.
* *heap-window*: the restart.

*Rationale.*
Per-hop peer identity cannot establish end-to-end restart order.
So the wire has its own incarnation rule, which trusts start order only where deployment declares it.

A persistent increasing source alone does not prevent old frames arriving after several restarts.
Where it applies, the strict older-incarnation refusal keeps those frames from moving the reference backwards.
Refusal finality requires proof of non-delivery, and an older incarnation is no such proof.
A false declaration that locks a client out produces counted refusal answers, so the lockout is never silent.
In the Report state, setup only records a client that is not declared increasing from a counter, so its starts are not known to increase, and the refusal is not selected there, where it could lock a live client out.

[NOTE]
====
This note states what follows from the rules of this item, of <<req-ssr-e2e-replay-window,A sliding window judges a reference's counters>> and of <<req-ssr-e2e-detects,The E2E wire detects each fault>>; it adds no rule.

* *A counter corrupted upward, undetected.*
The frame is delivered as a greater counter, and the window slides to it.
The sender's later frames then lie below the highest counter: on a request route those inside the window are delivered late and the others are refused as out of the window, and on a topic all of them are refused.
The sender's own frame whose counter equals the corrupted value is refused as a repeat, and its frames after that are delivered again.
A restart of the sender ends this too, as the run rule states.
* *An incarnation corrupted upward, undetected, for a sender declared increasing.*
The frame is delivered as a newer incarnation, which retires the live one, and the corrupted incarnation stays the newest adopted.
The live sender's frames are then refused until it restarts.
It is delivered again at once with an incarnation newer than the corrupted one.
With an older one, it is delivered again once a run of that incarnation is adopted, where the strict older-incarnation refusal does not apply; where that refusal applies, the requests of that incarnation stay refused until the receiver restarts.
* *Where the strict older-incarnation refusal does not apply.*
An incarnation older than the retired one can complete a run and become the reference.
Its late requests within their TTL can then execute, and a re-adoption can permit replay within the window (xref:../../safety/elements/up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-e2e-protection[The integrator decides which routes need end-to-end protection]).
====

[[req-ssr-e2e-replay-window]]
== A sliding window judges a reference's counters

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-replay-window~2",oft-title="A sliding window judges a reference's counters",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
*The window.*
For each declared sender on a request route or a topic, the E2E wire *MUST* judge, as the receiver, a frame of the reference's incarnation through a sliding window of 64 counters.

It *MUST* keep:

* the highest counter delivered;
* one bit for each of the 64 counters at and below it, set once that counter has arrived;
* a floor, the counter at which the incarnation became the reference.

When an incarnation becomes the reference, all its bits *MUST* be set.

*Judging a frame.*
A frame with counter c *MUST* be:

* above the highest: delivered, and the window slides; the counters that it skips open one gap (<<req-ssr-e2e-figures,The E2E wire's figures cover the stretch from encode to decode>>), and a counter that leaves the window without arriving is counted lost;
* inside the window and at or above the floor, with its bit set: a repeat, refused;
* inside the window and at or above the floor, with its bit clear: delivered late on a request route, and refused as late on a topic, and in both cases its bit is set, so that it is never counted lost;
* below the floor: refused, as below the floor; and more than 63 below the highest: refused, as out of the window.

A frame of the reference's incarnation *MUST NOT* be adopted: adoption is stated in <<req-ssr-e2e-resync,A restart resynchronizes, stale frames are refused, and no arrival order locks a sender out>>.

*When the reference changes.*
When the reference changes, by a restart or an adoption, the old window's counters that have not arrived *MUST* be counted lost at that moment.

*Cost.*
The window *MUST* change only under the slot's try-lock, in constant time, without heap and without waiting.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-replay-window~2+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the E2E wire
Verification:: test, miri, heap-window
* *test*
** Each case at its boundary: the window's two edges, the floor, a late request delivered and a late sample refused, a repeat inside the window.
** The window across a restart and an adoption, with its open counters counted lost.
** A counter that crosses 2^32^ is accepted.
** A loss of more than 2^31^ frames within one incarnation is delivered.
** A frame that reveals a gap is delivered.
** A repeat is refused, and named.
** A frame below the floor and one more than 63 counters back are refused, and named.
** A counter that has arrived and lies below the floor is refused as below the floor, not as a repeat.
* *miri*: the window's words under the slot's try-lock.

*Rationale.*
A bounded window can admit reordered requests once without accepting repeats.
A separate floor preserves the evidence needed for final refusal answers.

[[req-ssr-e2e-rpc-echo]]
== An E2E response answers exactly its call

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-rpc-echo~3",oft-title="An E2E response answers exactly its call",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire,SafetyUpRustCore"]
--
On an RPC route, the E2E wire *MUST* use the method header of <<req-ssr-e2e-header-format,The E2E header has one documented format>>.

*Requests.*
In each request, the E2E wire *MUST* carry the client's data ID as the Source ID, message type request, result normal and status 0.
A request that does not carry them is refused as corrupt, as <<req-ssr-e2e-detects,The E2E wire detects each fault>> states.

*Responses.*
In each response, the E2E wire *MUST* carry the server's data ID, echo the request's data ID as the Source ID, echo the request's counter and incarnation, and carry message type response, the response's result and its commstatus code inside the CRC.
A response has no sequence of its own.

*Acceptance at a client.*
At a client, the E2E wire *MUST* accept a response that meets all of these conditions, and *MUST* refuse every other response that it is given:

* its length, reserved bytes and CRC are intact;
* its type is response and its result agrees with its status;
* its data ID is a server the client declares;
* its Source ID is the client's own data ID;
* its incarnation is the client's own;
* its counter is the call's own, compared per call and not as a sequence over responses;
* its status is the response's commstatus.

*Refusal at a client.*
The E2E wire *MUST* name the cause of each such refusal: corrupt, undeclared data ID, misaddressed, stale, wrong call or status mismatch.
A copy that another server answers is refused as an undeclared data ID when the client declares one server for the method.

*Several declared servers.*
Where a client declares several servers for one method, an answer from another declared server to a misaddressed copy meets every condition, so the E2E wire accepts it: its check cannot tell that answer from the call's own (xref:../../safety/elements/up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-e2e-protection[The integrator decides which routes need end-to-end protection]).
The element's own checks of a response still apply (xref:../../up-l2/api.adoc#req-ssr-rpc-check-at-arrival[A response completes its call only once its wire accepts it]).

*A response with no E2E header.*
How the element refuses, names and counts a response with no E2E header on an RPC client's response route that selects the E2E wire is stated in <<req-ssr-e2e-refusal-counted,End-to-end refusals are named, and counted once>>.

*One response wire per method.*
On an RPC route that selects the E2E wire, the element *MUST* provide one response wire per method.

[[req-ssr-e2e-data-id]]
== One data ID per sender and route

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-data-id~5",oft-title="One data ID per sender and route",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire,SafetyUpRustCore"]
--
*Derivation.*
The E2E wire *MUST* ensure that each sender on a route has its own data ID, derived the same way on both sides from deployment data, by one documented function of the sender's URI, the route's resource ID, the message type, and the route's fixed sink where it has one: a notification's destination, a request's method.

*Acceptance.*
As a receiver, the E2E wire *MUST* accept only the data IDs declared for its route's senders (an RPC server's declared clients; a topic's publisher), and *MUST* refuse a frame with any other as an undeclared data ID, unless it refuses the frame as corrupt (<<req-ssr-e2e-detects,The E2E wire detects each fault>>).
How the element counts that refusal, and answers a request so refused, is stated in <<req-ssr-e2e-refusal-counted,End-to-end refusals are named, and counted once>> and <<req-ssr-e2e-refusal-answered,A refused request is answered, final or advisory>>.

*Construction.*

* The E2E wire *MUST* refuse at construction a collision among one receiver's declared senders.
* The element *MUST* provide the derivation as a function, so that the integrator's tooling and both sides compute the same ID.
* A declared sender given as a wildcard has no data ID, and the E2E wire *MUST* refuse it at construction.

*Responses.*
What a response carries, and how a client judges it, is stated in <<req-ssr-e2e-rpc-echo,An E2E response answers exactly its call>>.

[[req-ssr-e2e-sender-state]]
== E2E sending state is one counter per E2E value

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-sender-state~3",oft-title="E2E sending state is one counter per E2E value",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire,SafetyUpRustCore"]
--
*The E2E value.*
The E2E wire *MUST* keep its sending state in the E2E value: one counter and one incarnation per E2E value.

* The element's builders *MUST NOT* give one E2E value to two roles.
* An `+E2e+` value *MUST NOT* be cloneable, so it cannot be a transport default.
* A publisher, a notifier, and an RPC client on its request route each send on one route, so the value's counter is that route's.

*The counter.*

* The counter *MUST* start at 0 when its E2E value is constructed, so each incarnation's counter starts at 0.
* The counter *MUST* be 48 bits, and *MUST NOT* wrap: an encode at 2^48^ *MUST* be refused, by name.
* The counter *MUST* be kept in two 32-bit halves behind a try-lock.
* An encode that finds another encode of the same value in progress *MUST* be refused, by name, before the counter moves, so the receiver sees no lost frame.
* The lock sees a second sending thread only when two encodes overlap; the one-sender assumption of use still binds the integrator (xref:../../safety/elements/up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-e2e-protection[The integrator decides which routes need end-to-end protection]).
* The counter *MUST* advance by one per encode, so an encode or send that fails, or a send refused and then retried, shows at the receiver as one lost frame.

*The incarnation.*

* The incarnation *MUST* be 80 bits: 48 bits of the sender's start value, read once at construction, followed by 32 bits of the platform's initialization entropy.
* The start value *MUST* be the platform's wall clock in milliseconds, or a value the integrator supplies at construction, as the sender's deployment data chooses.
* Construction *MUST* be refused, by name, if the start value or the entropy cannot be read.

*RPC servers.*
An RPC server's E2E value *MUST NOT* read a start value or entropy: its responses echo their requests' counters and incarnations (<<req-ssr-e2e-rpc-echo,An E2E response answers exactly its call>>).

[[req-ssr-e2e-receiver-state]]
== E2E receiving state is one fixed slot per declared data ID

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-receiver-state~3",oft-title="E2E receiving state is one fixed slot per declared data ID",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
*One slot per data ID.*
The E2E wire *MUST* keep its receiving state in one fixed-size slot per declared data ID, in storage the caller provides at construction.
A slot *MUST* hold the state that <<req-ssr-e2e-detects,The E2E wire detects each fault>> and <<req-ssr-e2e-resync,A restart resynchronizes, stale frames are refused, and no arrival order locks a sender out>> judge by, its window (<<req-ssr-e2e-replay-window,A sliding window judges a reference's counters>>) among it, and the sender's figures (<<req-ssr-e2e-figures,The E2E wire's figures cover the stretch from encode to decode>>).

*Updates.*
The E2E wire *MUST* change a slot's state only under that slot's try-lock, and *MUST NOT* let concurrent decodes corrupt it.
A frame that finds its slot held *MUST* be refused and counted as contended, and *MUST NOT* be waited on, so no thread blocks.

*Reads.*
The figures are read without the slot's lock, as <<req-ssr-e2e-figures,The E2E wire's figures cover the stretch from encode to decode>> states.

*Route context.*
The E2E wire *MUST NOT* take route context: it judges a frame by the frame's bytes and by its own state, and a response at a client also by the call's tag (<<req-ssr-e2e-rpc-echo,An E2E response answers exactly its call>>).

*RPC clients.*
For an RPC client, the E2E wire *MUST NOT* keep a receiving slot: it checks a response against the call's tag (<<req-ssr-e2e-rpc-echo,An E2E response answers exactly its call>>).

[[req-ssr-e2e-refusal-answered]]
== A refused request is answered, final or advisory

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-refusal-answered~2",oft-title="A refused request is answered, final or advisory",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyUpRustCore,SafetyE2eWire"]
--
*The wire classes each refused request.*
For each request that it refuses, the E2E wire, as the receiver at a server, *MUST* state the first of these that applies:

* that the request is corrupt: its length, CRC, reserved bytes or method fields fail, so it has nothing trustworthy to echo;
* that its data ID was not declared;
* for every other refusal, its kind (stale, repeat, out of window, below the floor, contended, retired or older) and its class, final or advisory.

So a request that is corrupt is classed corrupt whatever data ID it names (<<req-ssr-e2e-detects,The E2E wire detects each fault>>), and gets no `+InvalidArgument+` answer.

*When an answer is final.*
The E2E wire *MUST* class a refusal as final in each of these cases, in which this server instance never delivered the request's tag and never will, and as advisory in every other case:

* in every state of strict mode, a request refused because its incarnation is not the reference, from a sender not declared increasing, whose counter is below `+resync_frames+` and whose incarnation is not the one the slot started with;
* where the strict older-incarnation refusal applies (<<req-ssr-e2e-resync,A restart resynchronizes, stale frames are refused, and no arrival order locks a sender out>>), also a request of the retired incarnation whose counter is above the highest that incarnation delivered, and one of an incarnation older than the reference, when none has been retired yet or when it is newer than the retired one.

*The refusal answer.*
For a refusal that has a kind and a class, the E2E wire *MUST* encode the refusal answer inside the method header and its CRC, with:

* the message result 2, refused and final, or 3, refused and advisory;
* the status `+Aborted+`;
* the refusal's kind, in the first reserved byte.

*The element answers.*
At a server, the element *MUST* give every request that the E2E wire refuses exactly one answer, except a corrupt one, to which it *MUST* give none:

* to a request whose data ID was not declared, `+InvalidArgument+`, sent as xref:../../up-l2/api.adoc#req-ssr-peer-rpc-undecodable-answered[An undecodable request is answered] states;
* to every other refused request, the wire's refusal answer, in every state of strict mode.
The element *MUST* send the class that the wire gave, and *MUST NOT* change it.

*Sending a refusal answer.*
The element *MUST* try a refusal answer once, whatever the response route's overflow policy, and *MUST* drop and count an answer that the route cannot take.

*Counting at the server.*
At a server, the element *MUST* count each request that the E2E wire refuses, under the refusal's name: corrupt, undeclared data ID, or its kind.
That count is the refusal's one count in the E2E counter (<<req-ssr-e2e-refusal-counted,End-to-end refusals are named, and counted once>>), and no second count of the request.
The count of an unsent wire refusal answer is stated under "Sending a refusal answer"; the count of an unsent `+InvalidArgument+` answer is stated in xref:../../up-l2/api.adoc#req-ssr-peer-rpc-undecodable-answered[An undecodable request is answered].

*At the client.*

* The element *MUST* complete a call at once on a final answer, as rejected with `+Aborted+`, naming the kind.
* The element *MUST* give an advisory answer back, so the call stays open for its real response, and a call that then ends at its TTL *MUST* name the kind of the last advisory answer that it got.

*The client's response queue.*
On a route that selects the E2E wire, the element *MUST* provide room in a client's response queue for one more frame per call in flight.

[[req-ssr-e2e-figures]]
== The E2E wire's figures cover the stretch from encode to decode

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-figures~3",oft-title="The E2E wire's figures cover the stretch from encode to decode",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyE2eWire"]
--
*The figures.*
The E2E wire *MUST* keep, for each declared sender and in its own fixed storage, figures that the application reads without taking the slot's lock, one atomic each, each saturating at 2^32^ − 1: lost, late and pending frames, gaps, restarts, resyncs, and refusals by kind.

* *Gaps* counts each run of counters that a delivered frame skips: the E2E wire *MUST* count one gap at once, when that frame reveals it.
* *Lost* counts each frame of the reference's incarnation that left the receiver's window without arriving, or was still open when the reference changed. So it lags the frame that reveals a gap by up to 63 frames.
* *Late* counts a first copy that arrived inside the window after a greater counter.
* *Pending* is a gauge of the gaps the window holds open now, kept in an atomic of its own.
* At each adoption of an incarnation at counter c, the figures *MUST* add c to frames *not delivered before adoption*.
** This is exact at that incarnation's first adoption; at re-adoption it can over-count.
** For counters from 0 through c - 1, *never arrived before adoption* is c less the distinct frames of that incarnation that arrived in that prefix, including arrivals refused before adoption and counting duplicates only once.
** The wire *MUST* report that count as exact only when its retained distinct-arrival history proves it, subject to saturation.
** When the necessary history is incomplete, it *MUST* report unknown, explicitly distinct from an exact zero, and *MUST NOT* present a partial count or estimate as exact.
** A cumulative figure with any unknown contribution *MUST* remain marked incomplete; a later exact contribution does not restore completeness.
* For a receiver's first frame, the figures *MUST* record where the receiver joined: that frame's incarnation and counter.

*History and completeness.*

* The history and its completeness metadata *MUST* use bounded, initialization-provided storage and add no runtime allocation, freeing or waiting.
* Lost history, including history evicted on candidate changes or an arrival that contention prevents recording, *MUST NOT* produce a false exact result or stop recovery or otherwise permitted traffic.
* The count and its completeness indication *MUST* be published and read consistently: concurrent publication *MUST NOT* pair an incomplete count with an exact indication.
* Saturation *MUST NOT* be represented as an unsaturated exact count, for any figure that the wire reports as exact.
* The lost, late and pending figures keep their own atomics and their own counting, and the history and its completeness indication change no replay or recovery rule.

*Use and limits.*

* Lost plus pending, and the gap count, show a loss when the frame that reveals it arrives; lost alone lags it by up to 63 frames.
* The figures do not count the lost tail of an incarnation, or frames sent before the receiver started.
* A frame that the receiving route drops or expires after decode is counted by that route and *MUST NOT* be counted in the figures.
* A frame counted lost that then arrives, more than 63 counters late, *MUST* also be counted as refused.

* *test*

** A late frame inside the window, counted late and not lost.
** A frame more than 63 counters late, counted lost and refused.
** Pending, read on another thread while gaps open and close.
** Not-delivered-before-adoption raised by c at each adoption at counter c, re-adoptions included, and exact at an incarnation's first adoption.
** Never-arrived-before-adoption exact when retained distinct-arrival history proves it.
** Prefix-boundary arrivals and duplicates counted correctly.
** An evicted duplicate, interleaved candidate changes or unrecorded contended arrival yielding unknown when necessary history is incomplete.
** Unknown distinct from zero.
** Cumulative incompleteness retained after later exact contributions.
** Concurrent count/completeness publication.
** Recovery continuing after history loss.
** A join's incarnation and counter.
** A restart.
** A resync.
** Each figure saturating.
** A frame that the receiving route's drop-oldest policy evicts after decode, counted by that route under its cause and not in the figures.
** A frame that the receiving route expires after decode, counted by that route under its cause and not in the figures.

*Rationale.*
A frame missing from a sender's counters is a certain loss only once the window has passed it or the reference has changed, and its gap stays pending until then.
The route counters of one hop and the E2E figures, which cover the stretch from encode to decode, answer different questions.

Distinct arrivals outside retained history cannot always be distinguished from duplicates.
So an explicit unknown preserves the observation limit without stopping traffic or requiring unbounded storage.

Completeness and saturation are different limits.

NOTE: Which figures an application reads to react within the fault tolerant time interval of the item, in ISO 26262's sense, is the integrator's to arrange (xref:../../safety/elements/up-rust/assumptions-of-use.adoc#aou-seooc-application-reacts[The application reacts to what the element reports]).

[[req-ssr-e2e-survives-hops]]
== Protection survives every allowed hop

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~ssr-e2e-survives-hops~2",oft-title="Protection survives every allowed hop",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="SafetyStreamer,SafetyE2eWire"]
--
*Carriage.*
The Streamer *MUST* ensure that a frame protected by the E2E wire passes that wire's check at the receiving endpoint after any allowed Streamer hop, also between legs whose envelopes differ or whose bindings adapt different middleware.
It does so by carrying the frame's payload bytes and its payload encoding identifier unchanged (xref:../../up-l2/streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-byte-identity[Carriage keeps the bytes]).

*Detection.*
Between them, the Streamer and the E2E wire *MUST* ensure that a byte of such a frame that is corrupted between hops is detected at the receiving endpoint.

* The E2E wire *MUST* refuse the frame as corrupt there, as <<req-ssr-e2e-detects,The E2E wire detects each fault>> states.
* The Streamer *MUST NOT* alter, replace or recompute any byte that the E2E wire's CRC covers, so that the corruption reaches that check.
The Streamer runs no end-to-end check of its own.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~ssr-e2e-survives-hops~2+`
ASIL:: B
Allocated to:: the Streamer; the E2E wire, for its check at the receiving endpoint
Verification:: test (frames carried through a test double of the Streamer's forwarding, between legs whose envelopes differ, and between legs whose bindings adapt different middleware, each passing its check at the receiving endpoint; the same through the ASIL Streamer, after each allowed hop), fault-injection (a byte corrupted between hops, detected at the receiving endpoint, the frame refused as corrupt)

*Rationale.*
A carrier forwards a frame's identifiers and payload bytes unchanged (xref:../../up-l2/streamer.adoc#req-ssr-streamer-byte-identity[Carriage keeps the bytes]).
So a check over those bytes at the receiving endpoint covers every hop before it.
The Streamer holds no part of the protection, so it can neither restore it nor vouch for it, and detection is the receiving wire's.
