= The owned-frame SDK contract
:toc: preamble

The key words "*MUST*", "*MUST NOT*", "*REQUIRED*", "*SHALL*", "*SHALL NOT*", "*SHOULD*", "*SHOULD NOT*", "*RECOMMENDED*", "*MAY*", and "*OPTIONAL*" in this document are to be interpreted as described in https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/bcp14[IETF BCP14 (RFC2119 & RFC8174)]

This area holds the ordinary obligations of an SDK that offers owned-frame routes, and of the parts that work with it, that no protocol chapter and no item of the safety area states.

[[req-sdk-owned-frame-contract]]
== The owned-frame SDK contract binds the parts that declare it

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~sdk-owned-frame-contract~1",oft-title="The owned-frame SDK contract binds the parts that declare it",oft-needs="uman",oft-tags="OwnedFrameSdk,OwnedFrameProvider,OwnedFrameScheduler"]
--
*The contract.*
The owned-frame SDK contract is the requirements of this area.

*Owned-frame routes.*
An owned-frame route is a route that an application declares on a transport that has an initialization mode and a runtime mode, as xref:../../safety/seooc-up-rust/runtime-modes.adoc#req-ssr-mode-two-types[Two modes, as two types] states for the element's transports.
On such a route, the storage that carries a frame, and each value decoded from a frame, has one owner at a time (<<req-sdk-owned-frame-one-owner,Frame storage and decoded values have one owner at a time>>).
The name "owned-frame" states that ownership rule.
It names no release generation, and it does not say that a payload is copied or serialized.
A route is native or serialized, and lent or handed off, as xref:../../safety/up-l1/payload-paths.adoc#req-ssr-payload-route-form[A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name] states, and each form keeps the guarantees that its own items state.

*Parts.*
An SDK is a xref:../../languages.adoc[language library] that offers owned-frame routes to applications.
Its core is the part that holds its synchronous or polled traffic operations, its two modes and its setup checks.
Where an SDK's core is the element (xref:../../safety/seooc-up-rust/README.adoc#req-ssr-element-boundary[The parts of the element, and element code]), the items of the safety area state what that core does, and an item of this area adds a duty for that core only where its statement gives the SDK one for its core.
A layer above the core is a part of the SDK, outside its core, that an application can leave out and that offers it operations on owned-frame routes or takes memory for them on its behalf, such as the application-role layer, the asynchronous layer or a layer that obtains startup memory from the host's heap.
Where the core is the element, a layer above the core is an optional layer, as xref:../../safety/seooc-up-rust/README.adoc#req-ssr-element-boundary[The parts of the element, and element code] names one.
The classic APIs are no layer above the core, and neither is a memory provider, a scheduler integration or an adapter.
A memory provider, a scheduler integration and an adapter are parts that the SDK ships or that another party supplies.

*Words of the safety area.*
Some items of the safety area define a word for the element or for a part that works with the element, such as a transport and its initialization and runtime modes, a binding and its middleware, a wire, a platform backend, a loan, a lease, a transmit loan, a retained frame, a decoded value, startup memory or a region.
Where an item of this area uses such a word for an SDK whose core is not the element, for its routes or for a part that works with it, the word means that SDK's own counterpart: what has the same role for that SDK.
Where an SDK's core is the element, such a word has the meaning that the safety item gives it.
A heap class, a route's declared policy, a native route and a guarantee that a route requires by name keep, for every SDK, the meaning that the safety area gives them.

*Whom it binds.*
A requirement of this area binds a part only where the part's declaration names the owned-frame SDK contract.
It then binds the actors to which its statement gives a duty, as xref:../../safety/README.adoc#req-ssr-requirements-bind[A requirement binds the actors to which it gives a duty] states of a requirement of the safety area.
Each such actor *MUST* meet each duty that the statement gives it, within the conditions that the statement states.
A component that a declaring part uses or wraps without a declaration of its own, such as a standard allocator, takes no duty of this area by that.
The duties of this area for what a declaring part supplies through such a component are the declaring part's.

*Offerings.*
Some statements give a duty for an offering, such as the selection of a memory provider, application roles, asynchronous operations on owned-frame routes, the classic APIs beside owned-frame routes or an adapter.
Such a duty binds a declaring part wherever the part makes that offering, whether or not its declaration lists it, and nowhere else.

*The declaration.*
A part whose declaration names the owned-frame SDK contract *MUST* state in that declaration, which is released with the part:

* which part it is, and which kinds of part it is: an SDK, a layer above the core, a memory provider, a scheduler integration, an adapter;
* the revision of the specification that its claims rest on;
* for each offering that a requirement of this area names as a condition, whether the part makes it.

A declaration is no claim.
The owned-frame SDK contract is no named profile of the safety area (xref:../../safety/README.adoc#req-ssr-contract-transition[What a part declares and claims, and when a replacement takes effect]).

*Claims.*
A component conformance claim to items of this area is a claim that a part meets items of this area.
In each such claim, the part *MUST* name its declaration and each item that the claim rests on, at the revision that the declared revision of the specification fixes.
A claim that rests on an item rests on that item's dependency closure too, as xref:../../safety/README.adoc#req-ssr-contract-transition[What a part declares and claims, and when a replacement takes effect] defines a closure.
Where a claim rests on fewer than all the requirements of this area that bind the part, the part *MUST* name in the claim each of them that it leaves out.
What a claim covers, what its evidence shows, what establishes none, and what a part says where one implementation cannot meet both an item and a rule of a protocol chapter, are as that item states for a claim of the safety area.

*The supplier.*
The supplier of each part whose declaration names the owned-frame SDK contract *MUST* release the part's declaration, and each component conformance claim made for the part, with the part.
The supplier *MUST* keep, for the items of this area that bind the part, the record that xref:../../safety/README.adoc#req-ssr-contract-transition[What a part declares and claims, and when a replacement takes effect] requires of a supplier.

*How the items are read.*
An item of this area is read as xref:../../safety/README.adoc#req-ssr-item-reading[How an item is read] states.
Every item here is a requirement.
None carries an ASIL: an item of this area states an ordinary obligation and makes no safety claim.

*Rules left in force.*
The contract adds to the rules that already bind a part, and replaces none.
No item of this area replaces, reallocates, retires or weakens a rule of a protocol chapter or an item of the safety area.
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~sdk-owned-frame-contract~1+`
Allocated to:: each part whose declaration names the owned-frame SDK contract: an SDK, a layer above the core, a memory provider, a scheduler integration or an adapter; the supplier of each such part, for releasing its declaration and its claims with it and for the record of where the part implements each item of this area that binds it
Verification:: review
* Each part's declaration: which part it is and which kinds of part it is, the revision of the specification that its claims rest on, and for each offering that a requirement of this area names as a condition, whether the part makes it.
* Each component conformance claim to items of this area: its declaration and each item that it rests on, named at the revision that the declared revision of the specification fixes, with their dependency closure, and each binding requirement that the claim leaves out named in it.
* Where one implementation cannot meet an item of this area and a rule of a protocol chapter: the part's declaration says so with the item's revision, the part claims no conformance to the chapter's rule for that behaviour, and no claim names both rules.
* That no component conformance claim to items of this area rests on a report's checks beyond what the report names, or on anything that xref:../../safety/README.adoc#req-ssr-contract-transition[What a part declares and claims, and when a replacement takes effect] says establishes no claim.
* Each supplier of a declaring part: the declaration and each component conformance claim released with the part, and the record of where the part implements each item of this area that binds it, with who made each entry, whether or not a component conformance claim is made for the part.
* Each offering that a declaring part makes, listed in its declaration or not, held to the duties that statements of this area give for it.
* Each component that a declaring part uses or wraps without a declaration of its own: the duties of this area for what the declaring part supplies through it, met by the declaring part.

*Rationale.*
An SDK, its layers, a memory provider, a scheduler integration and an adapter can be built and released apart, by different parties, and applications combine them in many ways.
The safety area defines words such as transport, binding and loan for the element, and another SDK has its own parts, modes and handles that do the same work, so the items of this area use the same words for them.
A heap class, a declared policy, a native route and a guarantee required by name rest on the element's own setup checks, so they keep their meaning.
A declaration says which contract a part follows, so whom an item binds is read from the part's declaration and the item's statement, and never from where the part came from.
An implementation that follows only the protocol chapters takes on none of these obligations, because no item of this area reaches a part without its declaration.
The routes of this area share one rule, one owner at a time, whether they are native or serialized, so the contract is named for that rule and for nothing else.
A claim that names its declaration, its items and what it leaves out, at one revision, is read against one exact text, and the supplier who releases the part answers for it.

[[req-sdk-owned-frame-one-owner]]
== Frame storage and decoded values have one owner at a time

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~sdk-owned-frame-one-owner~1",oft-title="Frame storage and decoded values have one owner at a time",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="OwnedFrameSdk"]
--
*The rule.*
On each owned-frame route that it offers, an SDK whose core is not the element *MUST* give the storage that carries a frame, and each value decoded from a frame, one owner at a time: from the moment that the storage is lent or reserved, or the value decoded, until that owner gives it back or ends it.
While an owner holds such storage or such a value, a forgotten owner included, the SDK *MUST NOT* let another owner reach it, reuse it or end it.

*Where the core is the element.*
For the element and its bindings, the items that keep this rule are xref:../../safety/up-l1/native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-in-place[A native payload is written and read where it is carried, or its route is refused], xref:../../safety/up-l1/bindings.adoc#req-ssr-binding-lent-storage[Lent storage is exclusive, disjoint and stable for as long as it is held], xref:../../safety/up-l1/payload-paths.adoc#req-ssr-payload-written-once[A payload is written once, into the storage that carries it], xref:../../safety/seooc-up-rust/release.adoc#req-ssr-release-lease-drop[A lease gives back its slot and nothing else] and xref:../../safety/seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-slot-types[A decoded value is destroyed once, as its route's class admits].
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~sdk-owned-frame-one-owner~1+`
Allocated to:: an SDK whose core is not the element, for each owned-frame route that it offers
Verification:: test, review
* *test*
** On each kind of owned-frame route that the SDK offers: a lent loan, storage reserved at setup and a decoded value, each held by one owner from its start until it is given back or ended, and none reachable through a second owner meanwhile.
** An owner that is forgotten: what it held neither reused nor ended, and reachable by no other owner.
* *review*: each path of the SDK that creates, passes or ends an owner of frame storage or of a decoded value.

*Rationale.*
The routes of this area are named for this rule.
One owner at a time is what lets a payload be written once into the storage that carries it, read in place, and ended exactly once.
Storage that two owners could reach could be written while it is read, or reused while it is held.
A forgotten owner still holds what it owned, so nothing that it held can be reused behind it.
For the element, the safety area states the rule for each part that keeps it, so this item binds only an SDK whose core is another.

[[req-sdk-owned-frame-classic-apis]]
== The classic APIs keep their contract beside owned-frame routes

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~sdk-owned-frame-classic-apis~1",oft-title="The classic APIs keep their contract beside owned-frame routes",oft-needs="impl,utest",oft-tags="OwnedFrameSdk"]
--
*The classic APIs.*
The classic APIs are the Transport Layer API of xref:../../up-l1/README.adoc[uP-L1], which sends and receives a xref:../../basics/umessage.adoc[`UMessage`], and the Communication Layer APIs of xref:../../up-l2/api.adoc[uP-L2], with their default implementation over it.

*Their contract.*
The protocol chapters that state them stay their contract, each key word with the strength that its chapter gives it.

*Additive.*
An SDK that offers the classic APIs beside owned-frame routes *MUST NOT* let the presence of owned-frame routes, of a layer above the core, of an adapter that the application has not selected for a call, of a memory provider or of a scheduler integration, in the same build or process, change the behaviour, the results or the defaults of a classic operation for the same input and the same configuration.

*No stand-in.*
The SDK *MUST NOT* serve a call of a classic API through an owned-frame route, or an operation on an owned-frame route through a classic API, except through an adapter that the application selects (<<req-sdk-owned-frame-adapter,An adapter keeps a message whole across a crossing, or refuses it by name>>).

*Runtime mode.*
What keeps a classic route out of runtime mode: xref:../../safety/seooc-up-rust/runtime-modes.adoc#req-ssr-mode-classic-excluded[Classic routes never enter runtime mode].
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~sdk-owned-frame-classic-apis~1+`
Allocated to:: the SDK, where it offers the classic APIs beside owned-frame routes
Verification:: test, review
* *test*
** The requirements-based tests of the classic APIs, with the same inputs and the same configuration, run with owned-frame routes, a layer above the core, an adapter that the application has not selected, a memory provider and a scheduler integration present in the build and in the process, and run with them absent: the same behaviour, results and defaults.
** A classic call with no adapter selected, beside a binding test double that records every use of an owned-frame route: no use recorded.
** An operation on an owned-frame route with no adapter selected: no classic API called.
* *review*: no path from a classic API into an owned-frame route, or back, outside an adapter that the application selects.

*Rationale.*
Applications and libraries written against the classic APIs rely on the behaviour, results and defaults that the protocol chapters state, with the strength of each key word.
If owned-frame routes in the same library or process changed any of them, those applications would break with no change of their own.
A classic message owns its payload, while an owned-frame route lends storage that has one owner at a time.
So a silent substitution in either direction would change who owns a payload, and for how long.
An adapter that the application selects makes such a crossing visible, and states what it does.

[[req-sdk-owned-frame-adapter]]
== An adapter keeps a message whole across a crossing, or refuses it by name

[.specitem,oft-sid="req~sdk-owned-frame-adapter~1",oft-title="An adapter keeps a message whole across a crossing, or refuses it by name",oft-needs="impl,utest,uman",oft-tags="OwnedFrameSdk"]
--
*An adapter, a crossing.*
An adapter is a part that carries a message between a classic API and an owned-frame route.
A crossing is one such carriage, in one direction.

*What it states.*
An adapter *MUST* state, in the documentation that is released with it, each crossing that it supports: its direction, the routes and messages that it accepts, each copy and each allocation that it makes, and who owns each value afterwards.

*Exact.*
In each crossing that it supports, an adapter *MUST* keep all five of these properties:

* _Identity._
The value of each attribute of the message, whether a payload is present, and a present payload's bytes and encoding identifier arrive as they were given.
No attribute value is changed, defaulted or dropped, and the payload is neither decoded, re-encoded, converted nor truncated.
* _Ownership._
Each value has one owner at a time.
On success, what the adapter took passes to the new owner that the adapter states for the crossing, or the adapter ends it exactly once.
* _Lifetime._
Nothing that one side receives refers to storage of the other side beyond its owner's hold.
* _Allocation._
The crossing makes only the copies and allocations that the adapter states.
It changes neither the form, the heap class nor a guarantee of the owned-frame route (xref:../../safety/up-l1/payload-paths.adoc#req-ssr-payload-route-form[A route's form is fixed at setup, by what the route declares and never by a name]; xref:../../safety/seooc-up-rust/heap.adoc#req-ssr-heap-declared[The heap class is declared, with no default]).
* _Errors._
The caller gets, for each outcome, a result that states that outcome.
A refusal stays a refusal with its cause, and a loss or a partial delivery is not reported as delivery (xref:../../safety/seooc-up-rust/errors.adoc#req-ssr-error-no-silent-loss[No silent loss, and each loss counted once]).

*Or refused.*
An adapter *MUST* refuse each of these crossings, with a result that names the adapter's refusal and its reason:

* a crossing that it does not support;
* a crossing to or from a native route;
* a crossing that would need a payload decoded, re-encoded or converted;
* a crossing that it cannot make while keeping all five properties.

The adapter *MUST* leave a message whose crossing it refuses owned by the side that gave it.
The adapter *MUST NOT* complete a crossing in a weaker form.

*Local handles.*
A loan, a lease, a retained frame or another handle that owns or borrows storage of an owned-frame route is a local ownership interface.
An adapter *MUST NOT* pass one to a classic API as a message or inside one.
An adapter *MUST NOT* build a classic message that borrows such storage.
A crossing from a frame of an owned-frame route to a classic message is a stated copy into storage that the classic message owns, or it is refused.

*Waiting.*
Which operations an adapter may offer as a nonblocking attempt or as a progress step: xref:application.adoc#req-sdk-owned-frame-operation-kinds[Each message operation is a nonblocking attempt, a bounded wait or asynchronous, and none hides a wait].
--

[horizontal]
ID:: `+req~sdk-owned-frame-adapter~1+`
Allocated to:: each adapter
Verification:: test, compile-fail, review
* *test*
** Identity: for each crossing that the adapter states, in each direction, the value of each attribute, the payload bytes, the encoding identifier and whether a payload is present arrive as given, a present empty payload and an absent payload included.
** Ownership: each value owned by one owner at a time; on success, each value that the adapter takes passed to the new owner that it states for the crossing or ended once, counted by a test double.
** Lifetime: nothing that one side receives refers to the other side's storage once its owner's hold ends.
** Allocation: the copies and allocations of each crossing, counted, against those that the adapter states, with the route's form, heap class and guarantees unchanged.
** Errors: a refusal, a loss and a partial delivery on one side, each reaching the caller as that outcome, with its cause.
** A crossing that the adapter does not support, a crossing to and one from a native route, crossings that would need the payload decoded, re-encoded or converted, and one that the adapter cannot make while keeping all five properties: each refused with a result that names the adapter's refusal and its reason, the message still owned by the side that gave it, and no crossing completed in a weaker form.
* *compile-fail*: a loan, a lease and a retained frame, each where a classic message is required.
* *review*
** The documentation released with the adapter, against each crossing that it supports.
** Each path from a frame of an owned-frame route to a classic message: a stated copy into storage that the classic message owns, or a refusal, and no classic message that borrows storage of an owned-frame route.

*Rationale.*
An application that moves a message between a classic API and an owned-frame route needs the same message on the other side, owned once, and valid for as long as it is held.
A classic message and a frame of an owned-frame route carry the attributes in different forms, so what has to arrive unchanged is each attribute's value.
A crossing that changed an attribute, re-encoded a payload or reported a loss as a delivery would make the two APIs disagree about one message, unseen by either side.
A classic message carries no agreement on a native layout, so a native payload in one could be read where no such agreement holds (xref:../../safety/up-l1/native-wire.adoc#req-ssr-native-agreement[A native type is read only under its exactly agreed layout]).
A loan or a lease owns storage of a route only while its holder keeps it, and a classic message owns what it holds.
So a handle inside a classic message would outlive the storage that it refers to.
A refusal by name tells the application which crossing to change, where a weaker crossing would hide the difference.

[[conventions]]
== Conventions and tags

[NOTE]
====
* *Identifiers.*
An item's identifier is `+req~sdk-owned-frame-<name>~<revision>+`, in its block attribute line and in its `ID` row.
Its anchor, `req-sdk-owned-frame-<name>`, holds its type and name without its revision.
* *Fields.*
After its statement, an item has an `ID` row, an "Allocated to" line and a "Verification" line, then its rationale.
* *Allocation.*
The "Allocated to" line mirrors the statement: it names each actor to which the statement gives a duty, with the qualifier that limits that actor's share.
Where the two differ, the statement governs (xref:../../safety/README.adoc#req-ssr-item-reading[How an item is read]).
* *Verification.*
A "Verification" line names methods of xref:../../safety/seooc-up-rust/README.adoc#req-ssr-verification-methods[The verification methods, and what a Verification line asks for], with the meaning that item gives them, and details them with cases.
It adds no duty.
* *Coverage needs.*
`impl,utest` marks behaviour in code, `uman` what a part states in what is released with it, and `impl,utest,uman` both.
====

NOTE: A tag is trace metadata: a selector by which a tracing tool chooses items.
It decides no actor, scope, status or conformance.
Every item here carries a tag of this legend, and no gate tag, no tag of the safety area and no tag of the protocol chapters.
